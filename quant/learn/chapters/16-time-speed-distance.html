Lesson 1 · Speed, units, average speed

In one line: speed is distance covered in one unit of time, and the average speed of a trip is total distance divided by total time.

1Picture this

You drive from Jaipur towards Delhi. After 2 hours your trip meter shows 120 km. In every single hour you covered 60 km on average. That "60 km in 1 hour" is the speed: 60 km/h.

Hour 1: 60 kmHour 2: 60 km

Three numbers always travel together: distance, speed, time. Know any two and you can get the third.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Speed tells you how much distance is covered in 1 unit of time.
60 km/h means 60 km each hour. So in 3 hours you cover 3 × 60 = 180 km.
Need time instead? Distance ÷ speed. 180 km at 60 km/h takes 180 ÷ 60 = 3 hours.
Units must match. km with hours, metres with seconds. Mixed units are the number one source of wrong answers.
Average speed of a whole trip = total distance ÷ total time. Not the average of the two speeds.
If you ride the same distance each way at two speeds, the slow leg takes more time, so it counts more. The answer lands closer to the slow speed.

3The rules (now that you get it)

Speed = Distance ÷ Time  ·  Distance = Speed × Time  ·  Time = Distance ÷ Speed
Average speed = Total distance ÷ Total time
km/h to m/s: multiply by 5/18 (same as ÷ 3.6). m/s to km/h: multiply by 18/5 (× 3.6).
Same distance each way at speeds a and b: average = 2ab ÷ (a + b)

Speed and average speed: Wikipedia "Speed". 3.6 km/h = 1 m/s: Wikipedia "Kilometres per hour". The 2ab/(a+b) form is the harmonic mean for equal distances: Wikipedia "Harmonic mean".

4Worked examples, one tap per step

A bus covers 150 km in 2.5 hours. Speed?

Step 1. Distance = 150 km, time = 2.5 h.
Step 2. Speed = 150 ÷ 2.5.
Step 3. 2.5 goes into 150 sixty times. Answer: 60 km/h.

Go to a town at 60 km/h and return the same road at 40 km/h. Average speed for the round trip?

Step 1. Pick an easy distance each way. Say 120 km (the answer does not depend on it).
Step 2. Time going = 120 ÷ 60 = 2 h. Time returning = 120 ÷ 40 = 3 h. Total time = 5 h.
Step 3. Total distance = 240 km. Average = 240 ÷ 5 = 48 km/h.
Check with the rule: 2 × 60 × 40 ÷ (60 + 40) = 4800 ÷ 100 = 48.

5The trap

Same question: 60 km/h out, 40 km/h back.
Wrong move: (60 + 40) ÷ 2 = 50 km/h. This assumes equal TIME at each speed.
But you spent 3 hours at 40 and only 2 hours at 60. So the answer must be below 50. Right: 48 km/h.

Lesson 2 · Relative speed and trains

In one line: when two things move, work with the gap between them: same direction, speeds subtract; opposite directions, speeds add.

1Picture this

You sit in a train doing 60 km/h. A second train runs on the next track in the SAME direction at 50 km/h. For you it creeps past slowly, at only 10 km/h. Now it comes the OPPOSITE way at 50 km/h. It whooshes past at 110 km/h.

10Opposite: 60 + 50 = 110

Same direction: 60 − 50 = 10. Opposite: 60 + 50 = 110.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Relative speed is how fast one object moves as seen from the other.
Same direction on a line: the faster one pulls away by the DIFFERENCE of speeds.
Opposite directions: the gap changes by the SUM of speeds.
A train passing a pole (or a standing man) must cover its OWN length. The pole has no length.
A train crossing a platform must cover its own length PLUS the platform length. Front reaches the platform start, back leaves the platform end.
Two trains crossing: add both lengths, and use relative speed. Then time = distance ÷ speed, in matching units (metres with m/s).

3The rules (now that you get it)

Same direction: relative speed = bigger − smaller. Opposite: relative speed = sum.
Train crosses pole: distance = train length. Crosses platform: train + platform.
Two trains crossing: distance = sum of lengths. Time = (L1 + L2) ÷ relative speed.

Relative velocity in 1D (difference when same direction, larger in magnitude when opposite): Wikipedia "Relative velocity". The train lengths rule is our own working from distance = speed × time, checked by Python.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

A 180 m train runs at 72 km/h. Time to pass a pole? Time to cross a 270 m platform?

Step 1. 72 km/h = 72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s.
Step 2. Pole: distance 180 m. Time = 180 ÷ 20 = 9 s.
Step 3. Platform: distance = 180 + 270 = 450 m. Time = 450 ÷ 20 = 22.5 s.

Trains of 120 m and 130 m run at 60 km/h and 40 km/h. Time to cross each other if (a) opposite, (b) same direction?

Step 1. Distance to clear = 120 + 130 = 250 m.
Step 2 (opposite). Relative speed = 60 + 40 = 100 km/h = 100 × 5/18 = 250/9 m/s.
Step 3. Time = 250 ÷ (250/9) = 9 s.
Step 4 (same direction). Relative speed = 60 − 40 = 20 km/h = 50/9 m/s. Time = 250 ÷ (50/9) = 45 s.

5The trap

180 m train at 72 km/h crosses a pole.
Wrong move 1: 180 ÷ 72 = 2.5 s. Metres were divided by km/h. Convert to 20 m/s first: 9 s.
Wrong move 2 (platform of 270 m): 270 ÷ 20 = 13.5 s. Left out the train's own length. Right: 450 ÷ 20 = 22.5 s.

Lesson 3 · Boats and streams

In one line: a river adds its speed to a boat going with it and takes it away from a boat going against it.

1Picture this

A boatman on the Ganga at Varanasi rows at 6 km/h in still water. Going with the current, which flows at 2 km/h, the bank slips past at 8 km/h. Coming back against the current, only 4 km/h.

Downstream 6 + 2 = 8Up 4

Same boatman, same effort. The stream decides the difference.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Boat speed means the speed in still water, written b. Stream speed is the speed of the water, written s.
Downstream (with the current): the speeds add. Speed over the bank = b + s.
Upstream (against the current): the stream pulls back. Speed over the bank = b − s.
This is the same idea as relative speed in Lesson 2: the water carries the boat like a moving train carries a walking man.
Everything else is distance ÷ speed, using the downstream or upstream speed for that leg.
Given the two speeds, work backwards: add them and halve to get b; subtract and halve to get s.

3The rules (now that you get it)

Downstream D = b + s  ·  Upstream U = b − s
Still water b = (D + U) ÷ 2  ·  Stream s = (D − U) ÷ 2
Time on a leg = distance ÷ (D or U, whichever matches that leg)

Adding velocities when one object moves on a moving medium: Wikipedia "Relative velocity" (man walking on a moving train, 10 + 40 = 50 km/h). The inverse formulas follow by adding and subtracting the two equations; checked by Python over many (b, s) pairs.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

A boat does 12 km/h in still water; the stream flows at 3 km/h. How long for 45 km downstream and back?

Step 1. Downstream = 12 + 3 = 15 km/h. Upstream = 12 − 3 = 9 km/h.
Step 2. Time down = 45 ÷ 15 = 3 h.
Step 3. Time up = 45 ÷ 9 = 5 h.
Step 4. Total = 3 + 5 = 8 hours. (Round-trip average speed = 90 ÷ 8 = 11.25 km/h, below 12.)

A boat goes downstream at 20 km/h and upstream at 12 km/h. Find boat and stream speed.

Step 1. b = (20 + 12) ÷ 2 = 32 ÷ 2 = 16 km/h.
Step 2. s = (20 − 12) ÷ 2 = 8 ÷ 2 = 4 km/h.
Check: 16 + 4 = 20 and 16 − 4 = 12.

5The trap

Boat 12 km/h, stream 3 km/h, 45 km each way (same example).
Wrong move: "the stream helps going and hurts coming back, so it cancels". Time = 90 ÷ 12 = 7.5 h.
But the slow upstream leg takes longer than the fast leg saves. Right: 3 + 5 = 8 h. A stream always makes the round trip slower.
