Lesson 1 · Area and perimeter (flat shapes)

In one line: perimeter is the walk around the edge, area is the number of unit squares inside, and every flat-shape formula is one of five you can memorise.

1Picture this

A rectangular plot in a village is 12 m long and 8 m wide. The farmer wants a fence round it (that is the perimeter) and wants to lay tiles on the floor of a hut built on it (that is the area).

Area 96 m² (tiles)Fence 40 m

Area: 12 × 8 = 96 square metres. Fence: 12 + 8 + 12 + 8 = 40 metres. Different units, different questions.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Perimeter = total length of the boundary. Its unit is a length (m, cm).
Area = how many 1-by-1 squares fit inside. Its unit is square (m², cm²).
Rectangle: rows × columns of squares, so area = length × width.
A triangle is half of a rectangle with the same base and height, so area = half × base × height. (Base = the side it sits on. Height = the straight-up distance from the base to the opposite corner.)
A trapezium has two parallel sides (they never meet, like railway lines). Average those two sides, then multiply by the height between them.
A diagonal is the straight line from one corner to the opposite corner. The sign √ means square root: the number that, multiplied by itself, gives the number inside. √289 = 17 because 17 × 17 = 289. Also a² means a × a (so 5² = 25).
Percent change of a side: +10% means × 1.10, −10% means × 0.90 (percent factors, chapter 04). Area uses two sides, so multiply two factors: 1.10 × 1.10 = 1.21, a 21% rise.
A circle uses the number π (about 3.14159). Many questions say "take π = 22/7".

3The rules (now that you get it)

Rectangle (sides l, w): Area = l × w · Perimeter = 2(l + w) · Diagonal = √(l² + w²)
Square (side a): Area = a² · Perimeter = 4a · Diagonal = a√2
Triangle: Area = ½ × base × height (height is at right angles to the base)
Circle (radius r): Circumference = 2πr · Area = πr²
Trapezium (parallel sides a, b; height h): Area = ½(a + b) × h

Formulas as stated on the Math Is Fun pages listed in Sources. a√2 means a × √2, about 1.41 × a.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Rectangle 15 m by 8 m. Find area, perimeter and diagonal.

Step 1. Area = 15 × 8 = 120 m².
Step 2. Perimeter = 2 × (15 + 8) = 2 × 23 = 46 m.
Step 3. Diagonal = √(15² + 8²) = √(225 + 64) = √289.
Step 4. 17 × 17 = 289, so diagonal = 17 m.

Circle of radius 7 cm, π = 22/7. Find area and circumference.

Step 1. Area = πr² = 22/7 × 7 × 7.
Step 2. The 7 on the bottom cancels one of the 7s on top, leaving 22 × 7 = 154 cm².
Step 3. Circumference = 2πr = 2 × 22/7 × 7 = 2 × 22 = 44 cm.

Trapezium: parallel sides 10 and 14, height 6. Area?

Step 1. Average of parallel sides = (10 + 14) ÷ 2 = 12.
Step 2. Area = 12 × 6 = 72 square units.

5The trap

A square has side 5, so area 25. The side is doubled to 10.
Wrong move: "side doubled, so area doubled to 50".
Right: new area = 10 × 10 = 100, which is 4 times 25. Area scales with the SQUARE of the length. The same holds for a circle: double the radius, area becomes 4 times.

6Mental-math shortcut: π = 22/7 with multiples of 7

Lesson 2 · Volume and surface area (solids)

In one line: volume is how much a solid holds, surface area is how much paint covers its outside, and cone and sphere formulas hang off the cylinder.

1Picture this

A carton (a box shape, called a cuboid) of tea packets measures 10 cm by 4 cm by 5 cm. How many 1 cm cubes fit inside? That count is the volume. How much brown paper wraps it with no overlap? That is the surface area.

Volume 200 cm³Paper 220 cm²

Volume = 10 × 4 × 5 = 200. Paper = six faces: 2 × (40 + 50 + 20) = 220.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Volume unit is cubic (cm³, m³). Surface area unit is square (cm², m²).
Names: cuboid = a box. Cube = a box with all edges equal. Cylinder = a tin can. Cone = an ice-cream cone. Sphere = a ball. a³ means a × a × a.
A cuboid is layers of a rectangle: base area × height.
A cylinder is layers of a circle: base area × height = πr² × h.
A cone with the same base and height as a cylinder holds exactly one third as much.
Surface area = add up every face. A cube has 6 equal square faces, so 6a². A cylinder: two circles (2πr²) plus one curved sheet that unrolls into a rectangle (2πrh), total 2πr(r + h).
A cone's curved side uses the slant height s (along the surface), not the straight height h. Cone total = base πr² + curved πrs = πr(r + s).
Space diagonal = corner to the opposite corner through the inside of the box. It is Pythagoras twice (the right-triangle rule is taught in Lesson 3).
Scaling: if EVERY length is doubled, area is ×4 and volume ×8. If only a cylinder's radius is doubled (height unchanged), V = πr²h gets ×4 because r is squared. If a solid is melted and recast, its volume stays the same.

3The rules (now that you get it)

Cuboid (l, w, h): V = lwh · Surface = 2(lw + lh + wh) · Space diagonal = √(l² + w² + h²)
Cube (edge a): V = a³ · Surface = 6a² · Space diagonal = a√3
Cylinder: V = πr²h · Curved surface = 2πrh · Total = 2πr(r + h)
Cone: slant s = √(r² + h²) · V = ⅓πr²h · Curved = πrs · Total = πr(r + s)
Sphere: V = (4/3)πr³ · Surface = 4πr²

Formulas as stated on the pages listed in Sources.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Cylinder, r = 7, h = 10, π = 22/7. Volume, curved and total surface?

Step 1. Volume = 22/7 × 7 × 7 × 10 = 22 × 7 × 10 = 1540.
Step 2. Curved = 2πrh = 2 × 22/7 × 7 × 10 = 44 × 10 = 440.
Step 3. Total = 2πr(r + h) = 44 × (7 + 10) = 44 × 17 = 748.

Cone, r = 7, h = 24, π = 22/7. Slant height, volume, curved surface?

Step 1. s = √(7² + 24²) = √(49 + 576) = √625 = 25.
Step 2. Volume = ⅓ × 22/7 × 49 × 24. Cancel: 24 ÷ 3 = 8 and 49 ÷ 7 = 7. So 22 × 7 × 8 = 1232.
Step 3. Curved = πrs = 22/7 × 7 × 25 = 550.

Sphere, r = 21, π = 22/7. Volume and surface?

Step 1. Volume = 4/3 × 22/7 × 21³ = 4/3 × 22/7 × 9261.
Step 2. 9261 ÷ 7 = 1323 and 1323 ÷ 3 = 441, so volume = 4 × 22 × 441 = 38808.
Step 3. Surface = 4 × 22/7 × 21². Here 21² = 441 and 441 ÷ 7 = 63, so 4 × 22 × 63 = 5544.

5The trap

A cube has edge 3, volume 27. The edge is doubled to 6.
Wrong move: "doubled edge, doubled volume" (54), or copying the area rule and saying 4 times (108).
Right: 6 × 6 × 6 = 216, which is 8 times 27. Volume scales with the CUBE of length.
Second trap: in a cone's volume use the straight height h, never the slant height s. For r = 3, h = 4, s = 5: right is ⅓π·9·4 = 12π, using s gives 15π (wrong).

Lesson 3 · Angles, triangles and Pythagoras

In one line: angles in a triangle always add to 180°, and in a right triangle the long side squared equals the other two squared added.

1Picture this

A 13 m ladder leans on a wall with its foot 5 m from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach? The wall, ground and ladder make a right-angled triangle, and one rule gives the answer: 12 m.

Ground 5Wall 12 (ladder 13)

Check: 5² + 12² = 25 + 144 = 169 = 13².

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

An angle measures the amount of turn between two arms. A full turn is 360°.
Acute is below 90°. Right is exactly 90°. Obtuse is between 90° and 180°. Straight is exactly 180°. Reflex is above 180°.
Complementary angles add to 90°. Supplementary angles add to 180°. Memory aid: C for corner, S for straight.
The three angles inside any triangle add to 180°.
Extend one side of a triangle outwards: the angle formed outside is an exterior angle. It equals the sum of the two interior angles (the angles inside the triangle, at the other two corners, called vertices) NOT next to it.
Angles in a ratio such as 2 : 3 : 4 (ratio is chapter 05): add the parts (9), divide 180 by that (20 per part), then multiply each part.
In a right triangle (one angle is 90°) the longest side (hypotenuse) is opposite the right angle. The two shorter sides that meet at the right angle are the legs. Rule: a² + b² = c².

3The rules (now that you get it)

Triangle: A + B + C = 180°
Exterior angle = sum of the two far (non-adjacent) interior angles
Complement of x = 90° − x · Supplement of x = 180° − x
Right triangle: a² + b² = c² (c is the hypotenuse)
Rectangle diagonal = √(a² + b²) · Square diagonal = a√2

Definitions and rules as stated on the pages listed in Sources.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Two angles of a triangle are 50° and 60°. Find the third angle and the exterior angle at the third vertex.

Step 1. Third angle = 180 − 50 − 60 = 70°.
Step 2. The exterior angle there = sum of the other two = 50 + 60 = 110°.
Step 3. Check: 180 − 70 = 110.

A ladder 13 m long has its foot 5 m from the wall. Height reached?

Step 1. The ladder is the hypotenuse: c = 13. One leg is 5.
Step 2. Missing leg² = 13² − 5². Here 13 × 13 = 169 and 5 × 5 = 25, so 169 − 25 = 144.
Step 3. √144 = 12 m.

Find the complement and supplement of 35°.

Step 1. Complement = 90 − 35 = 55°.
Step 2. Supplement = 180 − 35 = 145°.

5The trap

Hypotenuse 13, one leg 5. Find the other leg.
Wrong move: add the squares as if finding the hypotenuse: 5² + 13² = 194, √194 is about 13.93. A leg can never be longer than the hypotenuse, so this fails the sense check.
Right: SUBTRACT when a leg is missing. 169 − 25 = 144, leg = 12. Second trap: complement of 40° is 50°, not 140° (that is the supplement).

6Mental-math shortcut: Pythagorean triples

Whole-number right triangles appear again and again. If you spot one, you skip the square roots. Any multiple of a triple is also a triple.
