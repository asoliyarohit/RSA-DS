Simple Interest

In one line: interest is the rent you pay for using someone's money, and in SIMPLE interest the rent is always charged on the ORIGINAL amount only.

1Picture this

Ramesh in Kanpur borrows ₹10,000 from a co-operative society at 10% a year. Each year the society asks for 10% of ₹10,000, which is ₹1,000, every single year. It never asks for more than ₹1,000, because the base (the original amount the interest is charged on) never changes.

₹10,000 borrowed1,0001,0001,000

After 3 years the interest is 3 equal blocks of ₹1,000 = ₹3,000. The blocks are all the same size.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Principal is the money borrowed or invested at the start. Call it P.
Rate is the interest per 100 per year. "10% per annum" (also written p.a.) means ₹10 for every ₹100, every year. A "sum" in exam wording just means the principal.
Time is how long, in YEARS. Call it T.
One year of interest = P × R ÷ 100. For ₹10,000 at 10%: 10,000 × 10 ÷ 100 = ₹1,000.
Simple interest repeats the same amount each year, so for T years multiply by T.
Amount = Principal + Interest. The amount is what you hand back in total.

3The rule (now that you get it)

SI (simple interest) = P × R × T ÷ 100  (R in % per year, T in years)
Amount = P + SI = P × (1 + R×T ÷ 100)
Reverse: R = SI × 100 ÷ (P × T)  and  T = SI × 100 ÷ (P × R)

Simple interest means interest is not added to the principal for later periods: Wikipedia "Compound interest" (definition) and Wikipedia "Interest (economics)" (r·B·m/n form). The P·R·T/100 layout is the same formula with R written in percent.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Find the simple interest on ₹8,000 at 10% per annum for 3 years, and the amount.

Step 1. P = 8,000, R = 10, T = 3.
Step 2. One year = 8,000 × 10 ÷ 100 = ₹800.
Step 3. Three years = 800 × 3 = ₹2,400.
Step 4. Amount = 8,000 + 2,400 = ₹10,400.

₹6,000 grows to ₹7,800 in 3 years at simple interest. Find the rate.

Step 1. Interest = 7,800 − 6,000 = ₹1,800.
Step 2. Interest for 1 year = 1,800 ÷ 3 = ₹600.
Step 3. Rate = 600 ÷ 6,000 = 1/10 = 10%.
Check: 6,000 × 10 × 3 ÷ 100 = 1,800. ✓

5The trap

₹6,000 at 12% for 9 months. Tempting move: put T = 9 straight into the formula.
Wrong: 6,000 × 12 × 9 ÷ 100 = ₹6,480. That is bigger than the loan itself, which should make you suspicious.
Right: T is in YEARS. 9 months = 9/12 = 0.75 year. SI = 6,000 × 12 × 0.75 ÷ 100 = ₹540.

6Mental-math shortcut: percent per year as a fraction

Turn R×T into one total percentage, then use a fraction of P. Example: 8% for 2.5 years = 20% in total = 1/5 of P.

Use it. ₹4,500 at 8% for 2.5 years: 8 × 2.5 = 20%, and 1/5 of 4,500 = ₹900. Doubling time at simple interest = 100 ÷ R years (at 10%, 10 years). Tripling means a gain of 2P, so R×T must reach 200%: at 8 years that is R = 200 ÷ 8 = 25%.

Compound Interest, Growth and Depreciation

In one line: in compound interest, each period's interest is added to the money, so next period you pay interest on interest too.

1Picture this

Sunita keeps ₹10,000 in a bank deposit at 10% a year and never withdraws. After year 1 the bank adds ₹1,000, so she has ₹11,000. In year 2 the bank adds 10% of ₹11,000, which is ₹1,100, not ₹1,000.

₹10,0001,0001,100

The second block is bigger. That growing block is the whole difference between simple and compound.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Start with P. Interest for year 1 is P × R ÷ 100. Add it to P.
Adding 10% means multiplying by 1.10. So after year 1 you have P × 1.10.
Year 2 works on the new total. Multiply by 1.10 again: P × 1.10 × 1.10.
After n periods you have multiplied n times: P × (1.10)ⁿ.
"Compounded annually" means interest is added once a year. If interest is added twice a year (half-yearly), each period gets HALF the yearly rate and there are TWICE as many periods.
Quarterly: one quarter of the rate, four times as many periods. Depreciation means a value that FALLS each year (a machine, a car). It is the same idea with a factor below 1 (a 10% fall is ×0.90). Growth, like a town's population rising 5% a year, is the same with ×1.05.
CI (compound interest) = Amount − P. You find the amount first, then subtract.

3The rule (now that you get it)

Amount = P × (1 + R ÷ 100)ⁿ  (n = number of periods; R = rate PER PERIOD)
Compounded k times a year for T years: rate per period = R ÷ k, periods = k × T
CI = Amount − P  |  Depreciation: Value = P × (1 − R ÷ 100)ⁿ

Formula FV = PV(1+r)ⁿ and the "divide the rate by the number of periods per year" rule: Math Is Fun "Compound Interest"; A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt) and I = A − P: Wikipedia "Compound interest". The depreciation form is the same multiplication with factor (1 − R/100); it is derived in step 6 above, not taken from a fetched page.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Find the compound interest on ₹8,000 at 10% per annum for 3 years (compounded annually).

Step 1. Factor per year = 1.10. Three years: 1.10 × 1.10 = 1.21, then 1.21 × 1.10 = 1.331.
Step 2. Amount = 8,000 × 1.331 = 8,000 + (8,000 × 0.331 = 2,648) = ₹10,648.
Step 3. CI = 10,648 − 8,000 = ₹2,648.
Compare: simple interest would be ₹2,400. The extra ₹248 is interest on interest.

₹10,000 at 20% per annum, compounded half-yearly, for 1 year. Find the amount.

Step 1. Half-yearly: rate per period = 20 ÷ 2 = 10%.
Step 2. Periods = 2 × 1 = 2.
Step 3. Amount = 10,000 × 1.10 × 1.10 = ₹12,100.
Compare: annual compounding gives 12,000. More frequent compounding gives more.

5The trap

₹10,000 at 20% per annum, compounded half-yearly, for 1 year. Tempting move: keep 20% but use 2 periods.
Wrong: 10,000 × 1.2 × 1.2 = ₹14,400. That is a 44% gain in one year at a "20%" rate, which cannot be right.
Right: convert BOTH numbers. Rate 10% per half-year, 2 half-years: 10,000 × 1.1 × 1.1 = ₹12,100.

CI minus SI: the Difference Shortcut

In one line: the gap between compound and simple interest is only "interest on interest", and for 2 years that gap is P × (R/100)².

1Picture this

Two friends each deposit ₹5,000 at 10%. Anil gets simple interest, Bhavna gets compound. After year 1 both have earned ₹500. In year 2 Anil earns ₹500 again, Bhavna earns ₹550. The ₹50 gap is 10% of the first ₹500.

Simple yr 2: 500
Compound yr 2: 500+50

The whole gap is "interest earned on last year's interest".

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Year 1: simple and compound give the same interest, P × R ÷ 100. Call it I.
Year 2: simple repeats I. Compound earns I plus interest on I.
The extra in year 2 is I × R ÷ 100 = P × (R/100) × (R/100) = P × (R/100)².
So for 2 years: CI − SI = P × (R/100)².
For 3 years there is extra from year 2 (P × (R/100)²) and more extra in year 3.
Let x = R/100. 3 years of compounding multiplies by (1+x)³, which multiplies out to 1 + 3x + 3x² + x³.
SI for 3 years is 3x. Subtract 1 (the principal) and 3x: what is left is 3x² + x³. So 3 years: CI − SI = P × x² × (3 + x).

3The rule (now that you get it)

2 years: CI − SI = P × (R ÷ 100)²
3 years: CI − SI = P × (R ÷ 100)² × (3 + R ÷ 100)
Reverse: P = Difference ÷ (R ÷ 100)²  (2 years)

Built from the compound-interest formula A = P(1+r)ⁿ (Math Is Fun; Wikipedia "Compound interest") minus the simple-interest amount P·r·n. The shortcut formulas are our own algebra, checked by Python across many values of P and R. Use them only for annual compounding.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Find CI − SI on ₹5,000 at 10% per annum for 2 years.

Step 1. R/100 = 0.1, so squared = 0.01.
Step 2. Difference = 5,000 × 0.01 = ₹50.
Check the long way: amount = 5,000 × 1.21 = 6,050, so CI = 6,050 − 5,000 = 1,050. SI = 1,000. Gap = 50. ✓

Find CI − SI on ₹10,000 at 10% per annum for 3 years.

Step 1. x = R/100 = 0.1. x² = 0.01.
Step 2. 3 + x = 3.1.
Step 3. Difference = 10,000 × 0.01 × 3.1 = ₹310.
Check: amount = 10,000 × 1.331 = 13,310, so CI = 3,310. SI = 3,000. Gap = 310. ✓

5The trap

The 2-year formula is P × (R/100)². Tempting move: use it for a 3-year question.
Wrong: ₹10,000 at 10%, 3 years, with the 2-year formula gives ₹100. The true gap is ₹310.
Right: check the number of years first. 3 years needs the 3-year formula: 10,000 × 0.01 × 3.1 = ₹310. Also: these shortcuts are for ANNUAL compounding only.

6Mental-math shortcut: gap as a fraction of P

For 2 years, (R/100)² is a neat fraction of P. Learn the row, then divide.

Rate2-year gap3-year gap
5%P ÷ 4000.007625 × P
10%P ÷ 1000.031 × P
20%P ÷ 250.128 × P
25%P ÷ 160.203125 × P
Use it. ₹8,000 at 5% for 2 years: 8,000 ÷ 400 = ₹20. If the gap is ₹90 at 10%, then P = 90 × 100 = ₹9,000. If SI for 2 years at 10% is ₹1,000, one year is 500, so P = 500 × 10 = 5,000; the gap is 5,000 ÷ 100 = ₹50, so CI = 1,000 + 50 = ₹1,050.
