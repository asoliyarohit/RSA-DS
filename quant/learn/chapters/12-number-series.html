Number Series

Lesson 1. Gaps and ratios

In one line: a number series hides one rule, and you find it by subtracting neighbours first.

1Picture this

You climb a staircase in a Jaipur fort. Each step top is 5 cm higher than the one before. The step tops are at 7, 12, 17, 22 cm. You can say the next number without thinking. That is a number series: a list that follows a hidden rule.

7
12
17
22

Equal gaps each time: the rule is "add 5". Your whole job is to find the rule.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

A series is numbers in a fixed order. Each number is a term. A progression just means a series that follows a rule.
Rule one: look at the gaps (later term minus earlier term). Same gap every time? Then it is "add that gap".
If the gaps are not the same, take the gaps of the gaps. Gaps like 1, 2, 3, 4 grow by 1 each time.
If the numbers grow very fast, try ratios (later term divided by earlier term). Same ratio means "multiply by that ratio".
Ratios can also be 1, 2, 3, 4 (growing). Then each term is the last term times the next counting number. Example: 2, 2, 4, 12, 48: ratios 1, 2, 3, 4, so the next ratio is 5 and 48 × 5 = 240.
Write the gaps under the numbers on rough paper. The rule usually shows within 10 seconds.
One next action: subtract neighbours first. Always.

3The rules (now that you get it)

Arithmetic progression: constant gap d. Next term = last term + d.
Geometric progression: constant ratio r. Next term = last term × r.
The routine, same order every time: 1) gaps. 2) gaps of the gaps. 3) ratios. 4) squares and cubes (lesson 2). 5) primes, or sum of previous two (lesson 2). 6) odd and even positions apart (lesson 3). 7) × a, then ± b (lesson 3).

Definitions: Wikipedia "Arithmetic progression" and "Geometric progression" (see Sources). The 7-step order is our own suggested routine.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Find the next term: 7, 12, 17, 22, ?

Step 1. Gaps: 12−7 = 5, 17−12 = 5, 22−17 = 5.
Step 2. All gaps equal 5, so add 5.
Step 3. 22 + 5 = 27.

Find the next term: 16, 24, 36, 54, ?

Step 1. Gaps: 8, 12, 18. Not equal. Gaps of gaps: 4, 6. Not equal either.
Step 2. Try ratios: 24÷16 = 1.5, 36÷24 = 1.5, 54÷36 = 1.5.
Step 3. Multiply by 1.5: 54 × 1.5 = 81. (Easy way: 54 ÷ 2 = 27, then 54 + 27 = 81.)

Find the next term: 2, 5, 10, 17, 26, ?

Step 1. Gaps: 3, 5, 7, 9. Not equal.
Step 2. Gaps of the gaps: 2, 2, 2. Constant, so the gaps rise by 2.
Step 3. Next gap = 9 + 2 = 11.
Step 4. 26 + 11 = 37.

5The trap

Series: 2, 4, 8, 14, 22, ?
Wrong move: "2, 4, 8 doubles, so next is 16". But the 4th term is 14, not 16. The doubling idea broke.
Right: gaps are 2, 4, 6, 8. They rise by 2, so the next gap is 10 and the answer is 22 + 10 = 32.
Lesson: test your rule against every term given, not just the first three.

6Mental-math shortcut: chain the gaps

Write gaps in a row under the series. Then say them as a rhythm.

Gaps look likeMeaning

Use it. Ratio check: instead of dividing, ask "does ×2 give it? does ×3?". For 1.5: half plus itself. For ÷2 series, halve each time: 256, 128, 64, 32, 16.

Speed drill

Say the next term out loud first. Aim: under 10 seconds each.

Lesson 2. Squares, cubes, primes, Fibonacci

In one line: many series are a square, cube or prime list with a small twist, so learn the tables and compare.

1Picture this

A tiler in Pune lays square tiles in bigger and bigger squares: 1 tile, then 4 (2 by 2), then 9 (3 by 3), then 16. Each bigger square needs a new L-shaped border of tiles: 3, then 5, then 7. The totals are square numbers.

1
4
9
16

The gaps are 3, 5, 7: odd numbers. That is the fingerprint of squares.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

A square is a number times itself: 6 × 6 = 36. A cube is three copies of a number multiplied together: 3 × 3 × 3 = 27.
If the gaps are 3, 5, 7, 9 (odd and rising by 2), think squares.
Many series are a square or cube with a small change: n² + 1, n² − 1, n³ + n, n³ − 1. Here n is the position of the term: n = 1 for the 1st term, 2 for the 2nd, and so on.
To spot the change, find the nearest square (or cube) to the first term and see the difference.
A prime is a number greater than 1 that is not a product of two smaller numbers. 1 is not prime.
If each term is the sum of the two before it, it is a Fibonacci-type series.
One next action: compare the first two terms with the squares table below.

3The rules (now that you get it)

Gap between consecutive squares: n² − (n−1)² = 2n − 1 (odd numbers).
Prime: natural number (a counting number 1, 2, 3, ...) greater than 1, not a product of two smaller natural numbers. 2 is the only even prime.
Fibonacci-type: each term = sum of the previous two.
The routine, same order every time: 1) gaps. 2) gaps of the gaps. 3) ratios. 4) squares and cubes. 5) primes, or sum of previous two. 6) odd and even positions apart. 7) × a, then ± b.

Square, cube, prime and Fibonacci definitions: Wikipedia pages in Sources. Checked by Python for n = 1 to 999.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Find the next term: 5, 10, 17, 26, 37, ?

Step 1. Gaps: 5, 7, 9, 11. Odd and rising by 2: squares are involved.
Step 2. Nearest squares: 4, 9, 16, 25, 36. Check: 5 − 4 = 1, 10 − 9 = 1, 17 − 16 = 1, 26 − 25 = 1, 37 − 36 = 1. Each term is one more.
Step 3. Terms are 2²+1, 3²+1, 4²+1, 5²+1, 6²+1.
Step 4. Next: 7² + 1 = 49 + 1 = 50.

Find the next term: 2, 10, 30, 68, 130, ?

Step 1. Gaps: 8, 20, 38, 62. Growing fast. Compare with cubes 1, 8, 27, 64, 125.
Step 2. 2−1 = 1, 10−8 = 2, 30−27 = 3, 68−64 = 4, 130−125 = 5. The extra is n.
Step 3. Next: 6³ + 6 = 216 + 6 = 222.

Find the next term: 3, 4, 7, 11, 18, ?

Step 1. Gaps: 1, 3, 4, 7. Look: the gaps 3, 4, 7 are earlier terms of the series.
Step 2. 3+4 = 7, 4+7 = 11, 7+11 = 18. Each term is the sum of the two before.
Step 3. Next: 11 + 18 = 29.

5The trap

Series: 5, 10, 17, 26, 37, ?
Wrong move: "these look like squares", so next is 7² = 49. But 5 is not 2² (that is 4).
Right: every term is a square plus 1. So the next is 49 + 1 = 50.
Always compare the first term with the square: if it is off by a fixed amount, carry that amount through.

Lesson 3. Two-step rules, odd/even, missing and wrong terms

In one line: the gaps of a "×a ± b" series get multiplied by a, which reveals the rule in seconds.

1Picture this

A chaiwala in Kanpur sells 3 cups in the first hour, 5 in the second. Next hour 6, then 10. Two things are happening at once: the odd hours go 3, 6, 9 and the even hours go 5, 10, 15. Looking at the whole list is confusing. Looking at two lists is easy.

3 6 9 12
5 10 15 20

When the gaps jump up and down, split into odd and even positions.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Some rules have two steps: multiply, then add or subtract. Example: ×2 + 1 turns 3 into 7, then 7 into 15.
Hidden trick: for a rule "×a ± b", the gaps themselves get multiplied by a. In 3, 7, 15, 31 the gaps are 4, 8, 16.
So: if the gaps double, the rule is ×2 ± something. If the gaps triple, ×3 ± something.
Find the added number b: take the first term, apply ×a, and see what is needed to reach the second term.
If gaps jump up and down, split the series into odd and even positions and treat each as its own small series.
Missing term: find the rule from the side with more terms, then fill the blank. Example 4, 10, ?, 82, 244: 244 ÷ 82 is just under 3, so try ×3 − b. 4 × 3 = 12 but the next term is 10, so b = 2 (×3 − 2). Check: 82 × 3 − 2 = 244.
Wrong term: find the rule from the terms that agree with each other, then spot the one that breaks it.

3The rules (now that you get it)

Here t is any term, a is what you multiply by, b is what you add (or subtract). Rule: t(next) = a × t + b. Then each gap = a × the previous gap.
Find a = (second gap) ÷ (first gap). Then b = second term − a × first term.
Wrong-term: the terms after the wrong one follow the rule from the true value.
The routine, same order every time: 1) gaps. 2) gaps of the gaps. 3) ratios. 4) squares and cubes. 5) primes, or sum of previous two. 6) odd and even positions apart. 7) × a, then ± b.

Why it works: t₃−t₂ = (a·t₂+b) − (a·t₁+b) = a(t₂−t₁). Checked by Python for a from −3 to 5, b from −6 to 6. The 7-step order is our own suggested routine.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Find the next term: 3, 7, 15, 31, 63, ?

Step 1. Gaps: 4, 8, 16, 32. They double, so a = 2.
Step 2. b = 7 − 2×3 = 1. Rule: ×2 + 1.
Step 3. Check: 15×2+1 = 31, 31×2+1 = 63.
Step 4. Next: 63×2 + 1 = 127.

Find the next term: 3, 5, 6, 10, 9, 15, 12, 20, ?

Step 1. Gaps jump: 2, 1, 4, −1, 6, −3, 8. Split by position: odd positions are the 1st, 3rd, 5th, 7th terms; even positions are the 2nd, 4th, 6th, 8th.
Step 2. Odd positions: 3, 6, 9, 12. Add 3 each time.
Step 3. Even positions: 5, 10, 15, 20. Add 5 each time.
Step 4. The blank is the 9th term, an odd position: 12 + 3 = 15.

Find the wrong term: 5, 11, 23, 48, 95, 191

Step 1. Gaps 6, 12 double, so try ×2 + b. 5×2 + 1 = 11 and 11×2 + 1 = 23, so the rule is ×2 + 1.
Step 2. 23×2+1 = 47, but the series says 48. So 48 is wrong.
Step 3. Check: 47×2+1 = 95 and 95×2+1 = 191. Both match the true 47. Wrong term: 48 (should be 47).

5The trap

Series: 2, 5, 11, 20, ?
Wrong move: "2→5 is ×2+1, 5→11 is ×2+1, so next is ×2+1". That gives 20 × 2 + 1 = 41.
But 11 × 2 + 1 = 23, not 20. The rule broke at the third step.
Right: gaps are 3, 6, 9. Next gap 12, answer 20 + 12 = 32.
Test every pair before you trust the rule.

6Mental-math shortcut: gap ratio finds the rule

Write the first three gaps. Divide the second by the first. That number is a. Then b falls out.
