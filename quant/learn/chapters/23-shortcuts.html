Shortcuts and Tricks

In one line: these are the tricks coaching sites teach, but only the ones that survived a Python test, each with the exact moment it breaks.

How to read this chapter. "Coaching says it is fast" is their opinion. "It gives the right answer" is a fact only because we tested it in Python over thousands of cases. Every trick has a When it fails line. Read that line twice: it is where marks are lost.

Lesson 1 · Number-crunching tricks (DI, simplification, percentage, P&L)

In one line: swap, split, multiply factors, cross-multiply, round smartly, and check with digit sums.

1Picture this

A DI table says sales were ₹1,962 crore, and a product had ₹588 crore. "What percent?" Dividing 588 by 1,962 by hand takes a minute. Rounding both to 600 and 2,000 gives 30% in five seconds. The exact answer is 29.97%.

The trick worked here because both numbers were rounded up by about the same amount. Card 3 shows when that goes wrong.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

A shortcut is just a faster route to the same answer.
If it ever gives a different answer, it is not a shortcut. It is a trap.
So every trick below was tested in Python over a wide range of numbers.
Each trick has conditions. Outside them, it breaks. Learn the condition with the trick.
Open one trick at a time below. Read it, try the example, then stop.

3The tricks (tap one to open)

T1. Swap the percent: A% of B = B% of A
A% of B = B% of A

Example: 64% of 25 = 25% of 64 = 64 ÷ 4 = 16.

When it fails: never for plain "A% of B" (tested all A, B from 0 to 300). It only helps if one of the two is an easy percent (25, 50, 20, 10).

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. practicemock.com speed maths · guidely.in percentage tricks

T2. Fraction families and double-half
k/n as a percent = k × (1/n as a percent). Halve n's percent when n doubles.

Example: 1/8 = 12.5%, so 3/8 = 3 × 12.5 = 37.5%. 1/8 = 12.5% so 1/16 = 6.25%.

When it fails: the maths never fails (tested n = 1 to 50). The risk is rounding: 1/9 = 11.11% is rounded, so 5/9 = 55.56%, not 55.55%. Keep 2 decimals only at the end.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. oliveboard.in fraction-percentage table

T3. Split the percent (10% and 1% first)
10% = move the decimal 1 place left. 1% = 2 places. Build the rest.

Example: 62% of 740. 10% = 74, so 60% = 444. 1% = 7.4, so 2% = 14.8. Total = 458.8.

When it fails: never as maths. It fails in practice when you add a part twice. Write the parts down.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. pw.live approximation · practicemock.com speed maths

T4. Two changes in a row: a + b + ab/100
Net % = a + b + (a × b)/100. Use minus for falls and discounts.
Two discounts: a + b − (a × b)/100.

Example: discounts 20% and 10%: 20 + 10 − 2 = 28%, not 30%. Up 10% then down 10%: 10 − 10 − 1 = −1%.

When it fails: only when you forget the sign. A fall or a discount is a negative number. Tested for every whole a, b from −99 to 200. Only two steps: for three steps, use the formula twice.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. prepinsta.com successive discount · oliveboard.in profit and loss

T5. Compare fractions without dividing
a/b > c/d exactly when a × d > b × c (b and d positive).

Example: 17/23 or 22/31? 17 × 31 = 527, 23 × 22 = 506. 527 is bigger, so 17/23 is bigger.

When it fails: if a denominator is negative or zero. In DI, values are positive, so it holds.

Coaching-page technique (cross-multiplication for proportions), verified in Python. oliveboard.in ratio and proportion

T6. Round smartly (approximation)
For A ÷ B: round both the same way.
For A × B: round one up and one down.

Example: 588 ÷ 1,962 ≈ 600 ÷ 2,000 = 30% (exact 29.97%). 49 × 205 ≈ 50 × 200 = 10,000 (exact 10,045).

When it fails: when the options are close. If each number is rounded by up to 2% in opposite directions, the answer can be off by about 4% (Python worst case 4.04%). If two options are within 4% of each other, compute exactly.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. pw.live approximation · bankexamstoday.com DI tricks

T7. Digit-sum check (casting out nines)
Add digits until one digit is left. Digit sum of A × B = digit sum of (digit sum A × digit sum B). Same for +. For −, add 9 if needed.

Example: 238 × 47. Digit sums 4 and 2. 4 × 2 = 8. Option 11,186 has digit sum 1+1+1+8+6 = 17 → 8. Matches.

When it fails: (1) division: it does not work (10 ÷ 2: digit sums give nothing useful). (2) Swapped digits: 11,168 also gives 8. (3) Missing zeros: 120 and 102 both give 3. (4) Whole numbers only. Use it to cut options, not to pick one.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python (200,000 random pairs). theshortcutmaths.com digital sum

T8. Dishonest dealer (false weight)
Gain % = (true − false) ÷ false × 100

Example: sells at cost price but gives 900 g for 1 kg: 100 ÷ 900 = 11.11%.

When it fails: only when he sells at cost price. If he also adds a markup or loss, use (100 ± x) × true ÷ false − 100. Dividing by the true weight (100 ÷ 1000 = 10%) is the classic wrong option.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. oliveboard.in profit and loss

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Successive discounts of 25% and 20% on ₹1,200. Pay how much?

Step 1. Single discount = 25 + 20 − (25 × 20)/100.
Step 2. = 45 − 5 = 40%.
Step 3. Pay 60% of 1,200 = ₹720.
Check with factors: 1,200 × 0.75 × 0.80 = 720.

Which is larger: 13/17 or 16/21?

Step 1. Cross-multiply: 13 × 21 = 273.
Step 2. 17 × 16 = 272.
Step 3. 273 > 272, so 13/17 is larger (only just).

5The trap

Question: 588 ÷ 1,962 as a percent. Options: 29.97% and 28.9%.
Wrong move: round 588 down to 580 and 1,962 up to 2,000. You get 29%, and you might pick the wrong one when options are close.
Right: for division, round both the same way (600 and 2,000). The errors cancel and you get 30%, close to 29.97%.

Lesson 2 · Quadratic and number-series tricks

In one line: read root signs from the equation, find roots by "product and sum", and spot series by what the differences do.

1Picture this

Two auto-rickshaw meters. One always shows positive numbers, the other always negative. You do not need to read them exactly to know which is bigger. The sign rule does that for quadratics: sometimes signs alone settle "x vs y".

Quadratic equations were reported as 5 questions in 2025 (reported in memory-based exam analyses). Number series were reported as 5 in 2022 (same type of source).

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Write the equation as ax² + bx + c = 0 with a positive. If a is negative, multiply everything by −1 first.
Look only at the signs of b and c.
c positive: both roots have the same sign. b decides which: b minus means both positive, b plus means both negative.
c negative: one root is positive, one negative.
If both x-roots are positive and both y-roots negative, x > y. Done without solving.

3The tricks (tap one to open)

T9. The sign table
Sign of bSign of cRoots
++both negative
−+both positive
+−one each; the negative one is bigger in size
−−one each; the positive one is bigger in size

Example: x² − 14x + 45 = 0 is (−, +): both positive (5, 9). y² + 7y + 6 = 0 is (+, +): both negative (−1, −6). So x > y.

When it fails: (1) if a is negative and you read the signs raw (−5x² + x + 1 has roots 0.56 and −0.36, not both negative): make a positive first. (2) If there are no real roots (b² − 4ac < 0). (3) If b or c is 0. (4) When x and y roots have the same signs, signs alone cannot decide: solve. Python: 57,354 equations with a from 1 to 12, all matched.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. bankexamstoday.com quadratic sign rules · pw.live quadratic equation

T10. Quick roots: product a×c, sum b, flip signs, divide by a
Find p and q with p × q = a × c and p + q = b. Roots = −p/a and −q/a.

Example: 2y² + 3y − 5 = 0. a × c = −10. Pair: 5 and −2 (sum 3). Flip: −5 and 2. Divide by 2: −2.5 and 1.

When it fails: if no whole-number pair exists (the roots are not "nice"). Then use the formula or the sign table only. Tested for a from 1 to 9 and all pairs from −30 to 30.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. bankexamstoday.com quadratic tricks

T11. Both constants negative → "cannot be established"
If c/a is negative in both equations, the answer is always "relationship cannot be established".

Example: x² − x − 6 = 0 (roots 3, −2) and y² + 2y − 8 = 0 (roots 2, −4). x = 3 is above y = 2, but x = −2 is below y = 2. No single relation.

Why it is true: each equation has one negative and one positive root. The biggest x is positive, so it beats the smallest y; the smallest x is negative, so it loses to the biggest y.

When it fails: never, given both c/a are negative (Python: 100,000 random pairs). It only applies to the 2-roots-each "x vs y" format.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. pw.live quadratic equation

T12. Number series: one checking routine
1. Differences. 2. Differences of differences. 3. Do differences multiply (×a pattern)? 4. Squares/cubes/primes. 5. Split odd and even positions.

Verified facts behind it: (a) if each term is ×a + b of the last, the differences multiply by a every time. (b) If the terms come from n² or n³ formulas, the 2nd or 3rd differences are constant.

Example: 3, 7, 15, 31, 63, ? Differences 4, 8, 16, 32: each ×2. Next difference 64, so 127 (rule ×2 + 1).

When it fails: the order of checks is the coaching pages' opinion ("if gaps grow fast, test multiplication"). Mixed rules like ×1, ×2, ×3 or alternating + and × will not show in step 3; that is why step 5 exists.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python (20,000 random ×a+b series; 5,000 polynomial series). practicemock.com number series · cracku.in number series tricks

4Worked examples, one tap per step

x² − 9x + 20 = 0 and y² + 9y + 20 = 0. Relation?

Step 1. a = 1 for both (positive). Good.
Step 2. x: signs (−, +) → both roots positive.
Step 3. y: signs (+, +) → both roots negative.
Step 4. Every positive beats every negative: x > y. (Roots: x = 4, 5; y = −4, −5.)

Series: 2, 5, 4, 9, 8, 17, ?

Step 1. Differences: +3, −1, +5, −1, +9. No clean pattern.
Step 2. Split positions. Odd: 2, 4, 8. Even: 5, 9, 17.
Step 3. Odd positions double. The missing term is 7th (odd), so 16.

5The trap

x² + 11x + 30 = 0 and y² + 13y + 42 = 0. Both (+, +), so all roots negative.
Wrong move: "same signs, so cannot be established". Or guess "x > y".
Right: solve. x = −5, −6. y = −6, −7. The smallest x equals the biggest y (−6), so x ≥ y, not x > y.

6Mental-math shortcut: speed drill

Write the sign table on your rough sheet first. Then drill. Aim: under 5 seconds each.

  • "Find the wrong number in the series: 3, 7, 15, 31, 65, 127."
  • "What will come in place of the question mark? 4, 9, 19, 39, 79, ?"

Memorise this
1. Make a positive. Then c + : same signs (b − → both +, b + → both −).

Lesson 3 · Word-problem formulas (speed, trains, work, mixture, interest, ratio, ages, averages)

In one line: one ready formula per question type, each with the condition that makes it legal.

1Picture this

A Rajdhani at 72 km/h crosses a 150 m platform. The question is in metres and seconds. Multiply 72 by 5/18 and you have 20 m/s. The rest is one division.

Trains, time and work, partnership, SI/CI, ages and averages were each reported as 1 question in 2025 (reported in memory-based exam analyses).

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Each formula below is a normal method squashed into one line.
Squashing needs a condition: equal distances, annual compounding, same start time, and so on.
Before using a formula, find its condition in the question. If it is missing, use the long method.
Open one formula below, do its example, then stop.

3The tricks (tap one to open)

T13. km/h to m/s: × 5/18
m/s = km/h × 5/18. Back: × 18/5.

Example: 72 km/h = 72 ÷ 18 × 5 = 20 m/s. 18→5, 36→10, 54→15, 90→25, 108→30.

When it fails: never (1 km/h = 1000 m ÷ 3600 s). The answer is a whole number only when km/h is a multiple of 18 (Python, 0 to 999).

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. oliveboard.in trains

T14. Trains crossing
Time = (sum of lengths) ÷ (x + y) if opposite, ÷ (x − y) if same direction.
Pole or person: length of train only. Platform: train + platform.
Equal-length trains with pole times t₁, t₂: opposite 2t₁t₂/(t₁ + t₂); same direction 2t₁t₂/(t₁ − t₂).

Example: 120 m and 180 m trains at 50 and 40 km/h, opposite. 90 km/h = 25 m/s. 300 ÷ 25 = 12 s.

When it fails: mixed units (km/h with metres). Equal-length formula: only if the two trains really are the same length.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. byjus.com train problems · oliveboard.in trains

T15. Meet, then finish: speeds = √t₂ : √t₁
Two start at the same time towards each other. After meeting, first takes t₁, second takes t₂ to finish. Speed 1 : Speed 2 = √t₂ : √t₁.

Example: after meeting, 9 h and 4 h. Speeds = √4 : √9 = 2 : 3.

When it fails: if they start at different times, or speeds change. Python: 50,000 random cases matched.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. byjus.com train problems

T16. Average speed over equal distances: 2xy/(x + y)
Same distance at x and y: average = 2xy/(x + y). Same time at x and y: (x + y)/2.

Example: to office at 60 km/h, back at 40 km/h: 2 × 60 × 40 ÷ 100 = 48 km/h, not 50.

When it fails: if the distances differ. Then use total distance ÷ total time.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. geeksforgeeks.org speed time distance

T17. Time and work: LCM units, or ab/(a + b)
Total work = LCM of the days. Each person's units/day = total ÷ days. Two people together: ab/(a + b) days.

Example: A 10 days, B 15 days. LCM 30. A does 3, B does 2 per day. Together 5 per day: 30 ÷ 5 = 6 days (= 150/25).

When it fails: if someone leaves or joins midway (then add work done in parts), or rates change. Tested a, b from 1 to 60.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. oliveboard.in time and work · testbook.com time and work

T18. Mixture: alligation and replacement
Cheaper : dearer = (dearer − mean) : (mean − cheaper).
Remove b from a, refill, n times: original left = a × (1 − b/a)ⁿ.

Example: tea ₹30 and ₹50, want ₹36. (50 − 36) : (36 − 30) = 14 : 6 = 7 : 3. 40 L milk, take out 10 L and refill with water twice: 40 × (3/4)² = 22.5 L.

When it fails: alligation needs the mean strictly between the two values. Replacement needs the same amount removed each time and refilled with the other liquid. Warning: the cited Oliveboard page prints these two examples as 7:1 and 24.75 L. Python gives 7:3 and 22.5 L. This is why we test.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. oliveboard.in mixture and alligation

T19. SI/CI: 2-year and 3-year differences
CI − SI (2 years) = P(r/100)².
CI − SI (3 years) = P(r/100)² × (3 + r/100).
2-year CI rate = 2r + r²/100.

Example: ₹5,000 at 10%: 2-year difference = 5,000 × 0.01 = ₹50. 3-year = 50 × 3.1 = ₹155. 2-year CI rate at 10% = 21%.

When it fails: if compounding is not yearly, if the rate changes between years, or if SI and CI use different sums or rates. Tested r from 1 to 50.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. ibpsguide.com SI/CI shortcuts · geeksforgeeks.org CI shortcuts · 2r + r²/100 is T4 with a = b = r (practicemock.com)

T20. Ratio chain and partnership
A:B = a:b and B:C = c:d → A:B:C = a×c : b×c : b×d.
Partnership profit share = capital × time.

Example: A:B = 2:3, B:C = 4:5 → 8:12:15. A puts ₹30,000 for 12 months, B ₹40,000 for 6 months: 3,60,000 : 2,40,000 = 3 : 2.

When it fails: chain: never (tested 20,000). Partnership: capital × time is the rule the questions use; if a working partner takes a salary first, remove it before sharing. Keep time in the same unit.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. oliveboard.in ratio · oliveboard.in partnership

T21. Ages: the gap never changes, plus the ratio formula
Age gap is the same every year.
Now a:b, after n years c:d → one part k = n × (d − c) ÷ (b × c − a × d). Ages = a×k and b×k.

Example: now 3:4, after 8 years 7:8. k = 8 × 1 ÷ (28 − 24) = 2. Ages 6 and 8 (check: 14:16 = 7:8).

When it fails: if k comes out zero or negative, the data are inconsistent; re-read the question. For "n years ago", use −n. Tested every ratio with parts 1 to 9 and n from 1 to 20.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. ibpsguide.com ages · oliveboard.in ages

T22. Averages: replace or add one person
Replace one of n: new person = old person + n × (rise in average).
Add one to n: newcomer = new average + n × (rise).

Example: 8 men; a 60 kg man is replaced and the average rises 2 kg. New man = 60 + 8 × 2 = 76 kg.

When it fails: using the wrong n. Replace: n is the group size. Add: n is the old size. A fall in average is a negative rise. Python: 50,000 random groups.

Coaching-page technique, verified in Python. ibpsguide.com (average type)

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Two trains start together towards each other. After meeting, A takes 4 h 48 min and B takes 3 h 20 min. A runs at 45 km/h. Speed of B?

Step 1. Same start time: T15 applies. t_A = 4.8 h, t_B = 10/3 h.
Step 2. A : B = √t_B : √t_A, so B = A × √(t_A ÷ t_B).
Step 3. t_A ÷ t_B = 4.8 ÷ 3.333 = 1.44. √1.44 = 1.2.
Step 4. B = 45 × 1.2 = 54 km/h.

A class of 30 has average weight 40 kg. The teacher joins and the average rises by 1 kg. Teacher's weight?

Step 1. Add one: newcomer = new average + old size × rise.
Step 2. = 41 + 30 × 1 = 71 kg.
Check: 31 × 41 = 1,271; 30 × 40 = 1,200; 1,271 − 1,200 = 71.

5The trap

Rice ₹40/kg and ₹60/kg mixed to sell at ₹52/kg (cost). Ratio cheaper : dearer?
Wrong move: (52 − 40) : (60 − 52) = 12 : 8 = 3 : 2. That puts the cheaper rice's difference on the cheaper rice.
Right: the cheaper share uses the dearer gap: (60 − 52) : (52 − 40) = 8 : 12 = 2 : 3. The mean is closer to ₹60, so there is more of the dearer rice.
