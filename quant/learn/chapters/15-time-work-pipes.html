Time and Work, Pipes and Cisterns

Two lessons. Pipes are the same maths as work: a pipe is just a worker who fills a tank. Do lesson 1, then stop. Lesson 2 later.

Lesson 1: Time and Work

In one line: if someone finishes a job in 10 days, they do 1/10 of it per day, and workers together simply add their per-day shares.

1Picture this

Two painters are hired for a flat in Jaipur. Raju alone paints it in 10 days. Meena alone takes 15 days. Both start on Monday morning. Who finishes the flat faster than either one alone? Both of them together, obviously. But how many days?

Raju: 3 patches a dayMeena: 2

Cut the flat into 30 equal patches. Raju does 3 a day, Meena 2 a day. Together 5 a day, so 30 ÷ 5 = 6 days.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Call the whole job "1 job". It could be a wall, a report, anything.
If you finish in 10 days, then in 1 day you do 1/10 of the job. That per-day share is your rate.
Slow worker = small share per day. Fast worker = big share per day.
Workers at the same time: add their per-day shares. That is the combined rate.
Days needed = 1 job ÷ combined rate. Fractions are painful, so pick a friendly total number of "units" for the job.
LCM trick: choose the total units as the LCM of the days (the smallest number both divide into). Then each worker's daily units are whole numbers.

3The rules (now that you get it)

Work = Rate × Time. One worker's rate = 1 ÷ (days alone).
Together: 1/a + 1/b = 1/c, where a, b are the days alone and c is the days together.
Two workers shortcut: c = (a × b) ÷ (a + b).
LCM method: total units = LCM of the days. Daily units = total ÷ days. Days together = total ÷ (sum of daily units).

Rate = 1/time, rates add, and the 1/c = 1/a + 1/b form: Focus Tutoring "Combined Work Problems"; (a×b)÷(a+b) form: QuickMath "Combined work rate problems"; LCM definition: Wikipedia "Least common multiple". The LCM-as-total-units trick is just choosing a convenient unit, so the result is unchanged.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

A does a job in 10 days, B in 15 days. Together?

Step 1. LCM of 10 and 15 = 30. Say the job = 30 units.
Step 2. A: 30 ÷ 10 = 3 units a day. B: 30 ÷ 15 = 2 units a day.
Step 3. Together 3 + 2 = 5 units a day.
Step 4. 30 ÷ 5 = 6 days. Check: 10 × 15 ÷ 25 = 6.

A and B together take 12 days. A alone takes 20 days. How long does B alone take?

Step 1. LCM of 12 and 20 = 60 units.
Step 2. Together: 60 ÷ 12 = 5 a day. A alone: 60 ÷ 20 = 3 a day.
Step 3. B = 5 − 3 = 2 a day (subtract to find the missing worker).
Step 4. 60 ÷ 2 = 30 days.

A takes 12 days, B takes 18 days. They work on alternate days, A first. When is the job done?

Step 1. LCM of 12 and 18 = 36 units. A = 3 a day, B = 2 a day.
Step 2. A two-day cycle (A then B) = 5 units.
Step 3. 7 cycles = 14 days = 35 units. Only 1 unit is left.
Step 4. Day 15 is A's turn. A does 3 units a day, so 1 unit needs 1/3 of a day.
Answer: 14 1/3 days.

Wages. A (10 days) and B (15 days) finish a job and are paid ₹1,500 in proportion to the work each did. With 30 units, A did 3 per day and B 2 per day over the same days, so the work ratio is 3 : 2. A gets 1,500 × 3/5 = ₹900, B gets ₹600. The question has to say the pay follows work done; do not assume it.

5The trap

A takes 10 days, B takes 15 days. Together?
Wrong move: "average the days": (10 + 15) ÷ 2 = 12.5 days. Together can never be slower than the faster person alone (10 days)!
Right: 6 days. Rule of thumb: the answer must be smaller than the smaller time. If it is not, you have made an error.

6Mental-math shortcut: pick the total, split, add

Two habits. (1) Pick the LCM as total units. (2) For two workers say "product over sum": 10 × 15 ÷ 25 = 6. Start with small pairs where the numbers divide neatly.

8How the examiner asks

Typical style, written by us. Not copied from any paper.

  • "A can do a work in 15 days and B in 20 days. They work together for 4 days. What fraction of work is left?"
  • "A and B together finish in 12 days. A alone in 20 days. Find B alone."
  • "A is twice as efficient as B. Together they take 12 days. How long would A take alone?"
  • "A, B, C are paid in proportion to work done. Find B's share of ₹2,700."
  • Time and Work was reported as 1 question in the 2025 paper and 1 question in the 2022 paper in memory-based exam analyses (edutap). So expect about one, not a block.

Word clues: "twice as efficient" means rate ratio 2 : 1, so time ratio 1 : 2. "Alternate days" means a repeating two-day cycle. "Leaves after" means that worker stops adding units.

How the wrong options are built. The examiner plants the average of the days, the sum of the days, and the answer when the second worker is ignored. Remember: together must be smaller than the smaller time.

9Quiz · exam-style marks

Right = +1.25, wrong = −0.3125 (official). Guess only if your chance of being right is above 20%: 0.3125 ÷ (1.25 + 0.3125) = 0.2.

Lesson 2: Pipes and Cisterns

In one line: a pipe is a worker, an inlet fills with a positive rate, and an outlet or leak undoes work with a negative rate.

1Picture this

The overhead tank on your terrace in Patna. The motor pipe fills it in 12 hours. A tap at the bottom drains it in 20 hours. Leave both on. Does the tank fill? Yes, but slowly, because the tap steals part of the water.

Fills 5 an hourDrains 3

Tank = 60 units. In goes 5 an hour, out goes 3 an hour, net 2. 60 ÷ 2 = 30 hours.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

An inlet pipe fills the tank. An outlet pipe, or a leak, empties it.
A pipe that fills the tank in 12 hours fills 1/12 of it each hour. Same as work.
An outlet that empties it in 20 hours removes 1/20 each hour.
Pipes open together: add the inlets, subtract the outlets. That is the net rate.
Pick the LCM of all the times as the tank size in "litres". Then each pipe's litres per hour are whole numbers.
Time = tank ÷ net litres per hour. If the net is zero or negative, the tank never fills.

3The rules (now that you get it)

Inlet rate = +1/n per hour. Outlet or leak rate = −1/n per hour.
Net rate = (sum of inlet rates) − (sum of outlet rates).
Time to fill = tank ÷ net rate (only if net is positive).
Leak: a pipe fills in x hours, but with the leak open it takes y hours (y > x). The leak alone empties the full tank in 1 ÷ (1/x − 1/y) hours.

Inlet/outlet definitions, 1/n per hour, net work = inlets − outlets: GeeksforGeeks "Pipes and Cistern". Leak formula: same family of formulas as quoted in search results for pipes and cisterns; here we re-derive it from the net-rate rule and check it by Python.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Pipes A (12 hours) and B (15 hours) fill a tank. Pipe C empties it in 20 hours. All three open. Time to fill?

Step 1. LCM of 12, 15, 20 = 60 litres.
Step 2. A: +5 an hour. B: +4 an hour. C: −3 an hour.
Step 3. Net = 5 + 4 − 3 = 6 litres an hour.
Step 4. 60 ÷ 6 = 10 hours.

A pipe fills a tank in 6 hours. With a leak it takes 8 hours. How long does the leak alone take to empty the full tank?

Step 1. LCM of 6 and 8 = 24 litres.
Step 2. Pipe alone: 24 ÷ 6 = 4 an hour. With leak: 24 ÷ 8 = 3 an hour.
Step 3. The leak takes away 4 − 3 = 1 litre an hour.
Step 4. 24 ÷ 1 = 24 hours.

Pipes A (10 hours) and B (15 hours) both open for 3 hours, then A is closed. Total time to fill?

Step 1. 30 litres. A = 3 an hour, B = 2 an hour.
Step 2. First 3 hours: 5 × 3 = 15 litres. Half the tank.
Step 3. Left: 15 litres. B alone does 2 an hour: 15 ÷ 2 = 7.5 hours.
Step 4. Total = 3 + 7.5 = 10.5 hours.

5The trap

Inlets A (12 h), B (15 h), outlet C (20 h), all open.
Wrong move: add all three rates as if C also fills: 1/12 + 1/15 + 1/20 = 1/5, so 5 hours.
Right: subtract the outlet. 1/12 + 1/15 − 1/20 = 1/10, so 10 hours. Also never write "leak time = 8 − 6 = 2 hours": rates subtract, times do not.

6Mental-math shortcut: litres per hour, then net

Choose the LCM, convert each pipe to litres per hour, then add or subtract small whole numbers. You never touch a fraction.

Sanity check. With an outlet open, the time must be longer than the inlet alone. If you get shorter, you added the outlet.

8How the examiner asks

Typical style, written by us. Not copied from any paper.

  • "Pipe A fills a tank in 10 hours and B in 15 hours. If both are opened together, how long to fill?"
  • "Two pipes fill a tank in 12 and 15 hours. A third empties it in 20 hours. All open together. Time?"
  • "A tank fills in 6 hours, but a leak makes it 8 hours. How long does the leak take to empty the full tank?"
  • "Both pipes are opened, and after some hours one is closed. Total time?"

Word clues: "fills" means plus, "empties" or "leak" means minus, "closed after" means that pipe stops adding.

How the wrong options are built. Expect the answer from adding the outlet, the answer from ignoring the outlet, and the difference of times. Check the direction: outlet open means a longer time.
