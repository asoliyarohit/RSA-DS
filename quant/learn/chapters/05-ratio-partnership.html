Ratio, Proportion, Partnership

Lesson 1 · Ratio and proportion

In one line: a ratio is a recipe: scale every part by the same number, and to share a total, add the parts first.

1Picture this

Your family makes chai for guests: 2 cups of milk for every 3 cups of water. For 10 guests you do not change the recipe, you make more batches of it. That recipe is a ratio.

2 milk3 water

5 equal parts in all. Milk is 2 of 5 parts.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

A ratio compares two (or more) amounts. 2:3 is read "2 to 3".
Multiply or divide every part by the same number and the ratio stays the same: 4:5 is 8:10.
To share a total in a ratio, first add the parts. 5:3:4 has 5+3+4 = 12 parts.
Then find one part: total ÷ number of parts.
Then each share = its parts × one part. This is the whole method.
Two ratios are in proportion when they are equal: 6:9 = 14:21.
Direct: one goes up, the other goes up at the same rate. Inverse: one goes up, the other goes down so that their product stays fixed.

3The rules (now that you get it)

Share of a part = Total ÷ (sum of parts) × its parts
Compounding: A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5. Make B equal in both (3 and 4 → 12), then A:B:C = 8:12:15
Direct: y = kx (y ÷ x fixed). Inverse: y = k/x, so x × y = k (x × y fixed)
Proportion a:b = c:d means a × d = b × c

Ratio, proportion, direct and inverse definitions: Math Is Fun pages in Sources. The cross-multiply rule follows from equal fractions a/b = c/d (derived, Python-checked). Sharing a total by parts: derived, Python-checked.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Divide ₹7,200 among A, B, C in the ratio 5:3:4.

Step 1. Parts = 5 + 3 + 4 = 12.
Step 2. One part = 7,200 ÷ 12 = ₹600.
Step 3. A = 5 × 600 = 3,000. B = 3 × 600 = 1,800. C = 4 × 600 = 2,400.
Step 4. Check: 3,000 + 1,800 + 2,400 = 7,200. Answer: ₹3,000, ₹1,800, ₹2,400.

A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5. Find A:B:C.

Step 1. B is 3 in the first ratio and 4 in the second. They must match.
Step 2. LCM of 3 and 4 is 12. Scale A:B by 4 → 8:12. Scale B:C by 3 → 12:15.
Step 3. Now B is 12 in both. Answer: A:B:C = 8:12:15.

12 workers finish a job in 15 days. How many days for 20 workers (same speed)?

Step 1. More workers means fewer days. This is inverse.
Step 2. Product is fixed: 12 × 15 = 180 worker-days.
Step 3. Days = 180 ÷ 20 = 9 days.

5The trap

Two numbers are in ratio 3:5. A student adds 5 to both: 8:10. "That is 4:5, fine."
Wrong move: adding the same amount to both parts does not keep the ratio. 3:5 is 0.6, but 8:10 is 0.8.
Right: to keep a ratio, multiply both parts by the same number (3:5 → 6:10).

6Mental-math shortcut: simplify and share fast

Chunk 1: divide both sides by the biggest number that fits both. 18:24 → ÷6 → 3:4. 45:60 → ÷15 → 3:4.

Sharing shortcut. If the total is a multiple of the number of parts, one part is a whole number. ₹9,000 in 2:3:4 → 9 parts → one part ₹1,000. Before dividing, check whether the total divides evenly by the number of parts.

Lesson 2 · Dividing a sum and changes in ratio

In one line: turn every ratio into "parts", then use the gap or the total in parts to find one part.

1Picture this

Two cousins, Ravi and Meena, have savings in the ratio 3:4. Their uncle gives each ₹6 more pocket money. Does the ratio still stay 3:4? Think of two bars of different lengths. Adding the same piece to both makes the short one catch up, so the ratio moves closer to 1:1.

34

Adding the same amount to both makes the gap look smaller in comparison.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Write unknown amounts as parts: 3k and 4k. The letter k is just "one part".
A fact like "A has ₹60 more than B" is a difference of parts, not a total.
So find what the difference is in parts, then ÷ to get one part.
If the problem adds or removes the same amount from both, write the new ratio as a fraction and cross-multiply to find k.
For two mixed liquids, change only the one that changes. Work in litres first, then ratio.
Ratios with fractions (1/2:1/3:1/4): multiply all by the LCM of the denominators to get whole numbers.

3The rules (now that you get it)

Difference of two shares = (difference of parts) × one part
Total = (sum of parts) × one part
(a·k + x) ÷ (b·k + x) = p ÷ q → cross-multiply, solve for k
Fractions in a ratio: multiply by the LCM of denominators (1/2:1/3:1/4 × 12 = 6:4:3)

All follow from the ratio and proportion definitions in Lesson 1 (equal fractions, scaling all parts equally). Math Is Fun ratio page in Sources.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

A:B = 5:3. A has ₹60 more than B. Find B.

Step 1. Difference in parts = 5 − 3 = 2.
Step 2. 2 parts = ₹60, so one part = ₹30.
Step 3. B = 3 × 30 = ₹90. (A = 150.)
Step 4. Check: 150 − 90 = 60. Answer: ₹90.

Two numbers are in the ratio 3:4. Adding 6 to each makes the ratio 4:5. Find the numbers.

Step 1. Numbers are 3k and 4k.
Step 2. (3k + 6) ÷ (4k + 6) = 4 ÷ 5.
Step 3. Cross-multiply: 5(3k + 6) = 4(4k + 6) → 15k + 30 = 16k + 24.
Step 4. k = 6. Numbers: 18 and 24.
Step 5. Check: 24 ÷ 30 = 4/5 and 18 + 6 = 24. Answer: 18 and 24.

40 L of milk and water are in the ratio 3:2. 10 L water is added. New ratio?

Step 1. Milk = 40 × 3/5 = 24 L. Water = 16 L.
Step 2. Water after = 16 + 10 = 26 L. Milk stays 24 L.
Step 3. 24:26 = 12:13.

5The trap

A:B = 5:3 and "A has ₹60 more". A student does 60 ÷ 5 = 12 and says B = 36.
Wrong move: 60 is the difference (2 parts), not A's share (5 parts). The wrong answer is ₹36.
Right: 60 ÷ 2 = 30 per part, so B = 3 × 30 = ₹90.

6Mental-math shortcut: spot the parts fast

Chunk 1: always ask "what is the gap in parts?" before touching any number.

Use it. Ratio 7:5, sum 96. Parts = 12, one part 8. Shares 56 and 40, gap 16. Check: gap in parts 2 × 8 = 16. If the checks agree, your arithmetic is almost certainly right.

Lesson 3 · Partnership

In one line: profit follows capital × time, so multiply first and then share like any ratio.

1Picture this

Sunita and Farid open a small tailoring shop. Sunita puts in ₹50,000 and Farid ₹60,000. But Farid joins only 8 months into the year. At year end the profit is ₹36,000. Who gets more? Money matters, but so does how long it stayed in the business.

Sunita 50k × 12Farid 60k × 8

Think of "rupee-months": 50,000 × 12 = 600,000 and 60,000 × 8 = 480,000.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

In a partnership, partners put in money (capital) and share the profit or loss.
If everyone invests for the same time, profit is shared in the ratio of capitals.
If times differ, one rupee kept for 12 months earns more than a rupee kept for 6 months.
So multiply capital × months for each partner. This is the partner's "equivalent capital".
Share the profit in the ratio of these products. Then it is just Lesson 1 sharing.
A working partner manages the business; a sleeping partner only invests. The question will tell you if the working partner gets a fixed fee or commission first. Pay that first, then share the rest.

3The rules (now that you get it)

Profit ratio = (capital × time) of A : (capital × time) of B : ...
Same time for all: profit ratio = capital ratio
Same capital for all: profit ratio = time ratio (derived from capital × time)
Loss is shared in the same ratio as profit
Working partner fee or commission: only if the question states it. Deduct first, then split the remainder.

Capital × time rule, loss shared like profit, working and sleeping partner meanings: IndiaBIX partnership formulas page; Wikipedia "Partnership" (silent/sleeping partner shares profit and loss but does not manage). The fee-first step is an assumption written into each question, not a general law.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

A invests ₹50,000 for 12 months, B invests ₹60,000 for 8 months. Profit ₹36,000. Find each share.

Step 1. A: 50,000 × 12 = 600,000. B: 60,000 × 8 = 480,000.
Step 2. Ratio 600,000 : 480,000 = 5:4 (divide by 120,000).
Step 3. Parts = 9. One part = 36,000 ÷ 9 = 4,000.
Step 4. A = 5 × 4,000 = ₹20,000. B = 4 × 4,000 = ₹16,000. Check: sum 36,000.

A puts in ₹30,000 for the full year. B joins after 4 months with ₹40,000. Profit at year end ₹34,000. B's share?

Step 1. A: 30,000 × 12 = 360,000. B stays 12 − 4 = 8 months: 40,000 × 8 = 320,000.
Step 2. 360,000 : 320,000 = 9:8.
Step 3. One part = 34,000 ÷ 17 = 2,000. A = 18,000. B = 16,000.
Answer: B gets ₹16,000.

A (working) invests ₹40,000, B (sleeping) invests ₹60,000, for a year. Profit ₹90,000. A first takes 10% of profit as a fee for managing. How much does A get in all?

Step 1. Fee = 10% of 90,000 = 9,000.
Step 2. Remaining = 90,000 − 9,000 = 81,000, shared in capitals 40:60 = 2:3.
Step 3. A's share of 81,000 = 2/5 × 81,000 = 32,400. B = 48,600.
Step 4. A total = 9,000 + 32,400 = ₹41,400. Check: 41,400 + 48,600 = 90,000.

5The trap

A: ₹50,000 for 12 months. B: ₹60,000 for 8 months. Profit ₹36,000.
Wrong move: split by capital only, 5:6. A gets 36,000 × 5 ÷ 11 = ₹16,363.64. Time ignored.
Right: capital × time gives 5:4 and A gets ₹20,000. Longer stay wins.

6Mental-math shortcut: shrink before you multiply

Chunk 1: drop common zeros and factors before comparing. 50,000 × 12 : 60,000 × 8 → 5 × 12 : 6 × 8 = 60:48 = 5:4.

Use it. A 8,000 for 6 months, B 6,000 for 8 months: 8 × 6 = 48 and 6 × 8 = 48, so 1:1. Spotting an equal product saves all the arithmetic.

7Lab: two-partner profit split

8How the examiner asks

Typical style, written by us. Not copied from any paper.

  • "A and B start a business with ₹x and ₹y. After m months C joins with ₹z. Find the share of C in a profit of ₹P."
  • "A is a working partner and gets p% of profit for managing. The rest is divided in the ratio of capitals."
  • "If the profit is ₹P and A's share is ₹Q, find B's investment."
  • Partnership was reported as 1 question in both the 2022 and 2025 papers in memory-based exam analyses (edutap).

Word clues: for a 1-year business, "joins after m months" means time = 12 − m. "Withdraws after" means shorter time. "Sleeping partner" means capital only.

How the wrong options are built. Planted answers: capital-only split, the other partner's share, shares from the flipped ratio, and a fee-less split.
