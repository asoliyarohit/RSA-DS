Priority Map

In one line: with 12 days left, study the topics that keep showing up first, then the one-mark topics, and leave the rare ones for last.

1Picture this

You have 12 days and a long list. Think of a cricket innings with 20 balls: you do not play the rare ball first. You first learn to hit the ball that comes most often. The past-paper counts below tell you which balls come most often.

Honest warning. These counts come from coaching-site "memory-based" analyses, not from official papers. Sites disagree by a few questions. Two PFRDA years is a hint, not a forecast. ("Memory-based" = candidates recall the questions after the exam and a coaching site counts them.)

Topic names, one line each (each has its own chapter later):
Data Interpretation (DI) = answer questions by reading a table or chart (ch 19-20). A caselet is DI given as a paragraph instead of a table.
Number Series = find the missing or wrong number in a pattern (ch 12).
Quadratic Equations = equations with an x² term; you find x and compare (ch 13).
Approximation/Simplification = work out a long sum quickly, often by rounding (ch 11).
Quantity Comparison and Data Sufficiency = compare two values / decide if the given facts are enough (ch 14, 21).
SI/CI = Simple/Compound Interest (ch 08). Time-Speed-Distance includes Trains (ch 16).

2What the papers show, one exam at a time

PFRDA 2022 (5 Nov, reported in memory-based exam analyses): Number Series 5, Data Interpretation (DI) 5, Averages 3, Time-Speed-Distance 2, then 1 each of Profit & Loss, Partnership, Time & Work, Interest, Percentage = 20. (practicemock; edutap agrees on the top three; ixambee reports series as 4.)
PFRDA 2025 (6 Sep, memory-based): DI 8 (5+3), Quadratic Equations 5, then 1 each of Partnership, SI/CI, Trains, Time & Work, Rectangle, Ages, Average = 20. (edutap, practicemock identical.) Sources differ: Oliveboard gives a shorter, incomplete split (DI 5, Quadratic 5, and single questions on speed, ages, partnership).
Pattern in both PFRDA years: the top 2 or 3 topics made 13 of 20 questions: 5+5+3 in 2022 and 8+5 in 2025. As a percentage: 13 ÷ 20 = 0.65, and × 100 gives 65%. (Share of 20 = count ÷ 20 × 100.) Only two years, so treat it as a hint.
Other exams with a similar maths section (useful extra evidence, because PFRDA has only two years): IRDAI (insurance regulator), RBI (Reserve Bank) and SEBI (markets regulator) also recruit officers by an online test with a Quant section.
IRDAI Assistant Manager (2017, 2023, 2024 only; 40 quant questions): DI about 14 to 20, arithmetic about 10 to 13, simplification/approximation 4 to 6, number series 2 to 5, quadratic 0 to 4. Sources differ on exact counts by several questions.
RBI Grade B Phase 1 (30 quant questions, 2018 to 2026, no exam in 2020): DI including caselets 7 to 17 per year, number series 3 to 5, the rest rotates (quantity comparison, quadratic, data sufficiency, single arithmetic questions). Sources differ on most years, strongly for 2021.
SEBI Grade A Phase 1 (20 quant questions; years with fetched counts: 2022, 2024 and the exam held 10 Jan 2026): DI about 7 to 10 of 20 in 2024 and the Jan-2026 exam, number series 3 to 5, and either quadratic equations (2 to 4) or approximation (5 in the Jan-2026 exam) taking turns. Sources differ on 2022, 2024 and Jan-2026 counts, and 2023 and earlier years are not covered.
One clear signal across all four bodies: Data Interpretation is the biggest block wherever counts exist. Number series and quadratic equations come next, taking turns (approximation joins in at SEBI, IRDAI and some RBI years). Single arithmetic questions are mostly 1 each.

6The trap

Trap: "Quant is 25 marks, so I should try all 20 questions."
Maths: each wrong answer costs 0.3125, each right gives 1.25. Break-even chance of being right is 0.3125 ÷ (1.25 + 0.3125) = 20%.
So guessing blindly is only worth it when your chance is above 20%. If you can cross out an option, the odds usually improve. Skipping a question you cannot start is a legitimate choice.

7How the examiner asks

Typical style, written by us. This chapter has no exam topic of its own; it tells you which question types to expect most.

  • DI: "Study the table/chart and answer the questions that follow" (a set of 3 to 5 questions on one chart).
  • Quadratic: "Solve the two equations I and II and compare x and y."
  • Series: "Find the missing number" or "find the wrong number in the series".
