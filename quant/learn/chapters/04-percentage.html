Percentage

In one line: a percentage is "out of 100", and almost every exam question is just "compare with the OLD number".

1Picture this

A shop in Lucknow marks a shirt at ₹800 with a board: "15% off". Picture the ₹800 as a bar cut into 100 equal slices. 15% off means 15 of those slices are crossed out.

85 slices you pay15

One slice = ₹8 (800 ÷ 100). So the discount is 15 × 8 = ₹120 and you pay ₹680.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

"Percent" means "per 100". So 1% is 1 out of 100.
50% is 50 out of 100, which is half. So 50% = 1/2.
To find a percent of a number: percent over 100, then multiply. 25% of 80 = 25/100 × 80 = 20. (Shortcut: 25% = 1/4, and 80 ÷ 4 = 20.)
Percentage change asks: how far did it move, compared with where it started?
Always divide by the old (starting) value. That starting value is called the base. This one habit prevents most mistakes.
Two changes in a row (called successive changes) do not add. The second change works on the already-changed number.
Easy way: use a factor, the number you multiply by. Up 20% means 100% + 20% = 120% = 1.20. Down 20% means 100% − 20% = 80% = 0.80. Down 10% is 0.90. Multiply the factors to get the net (overall) result.

3The rules (now that you get it)

X% of N = (X ÷ 100) × N
% change = (New − Old) ÷ Old × 100
Successive changes: multiply the factors. Factor = 1 + change ÷ 100: +20% is 1.20, −20% is 0.80.

Definition of percentage and relative change: Wikipedia "Percentage"; Math Is Fun "Percentage Change" (see Sources). Changes do not add: Wikipedia "Percentage" (+10% then −10% ≠ 0). Multiply-the-factors rule: derived, Python-checked.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

A bag goes from ₹80 to ₹100. Percentage increase?

Step 1. Old = 80, New = 100.
Step 2. Change = 100 − 80 = 20.
Step 3. Divide by the OLD: 20 ÷ 80 = 1/4.
Step 4. 1/4 = 25%. Answer: 25% increase.

Price rises 20%, then falls 20%. Net change?

Step 1. Start at 100. +20% gives 120.
Step 2. −20% of 120: 20% = 1/5, and 120 ÷ 5 = 24. So 120 − 24 = 96.
Step 3. 96 versus 100 is −4. Answer: 4% decrease, not 0%.
Check with factors: 1.20 × 0.80. Ignore the points: 12 × 8 = 96. Two decimal places in all, so 0.96.

5The trap

Price goes 100 → 125. That is +25%.
Wrong move: "so it must fall 25% to come back". 25% of 125 is 125 ÷ 4 = 31.25, and 125 − 31.25 = 93.75. Not 100.
Right: it must fall 25 out of 125 = 1/5 = 20%. Going down uses the new, bigger base.
Same idea in shop questions. Money spent = price × quantity. If the price goes up 25% (factor 1.25), the quantity must be multiplied by 0.80 to keep the spend the same (1.25 × 0.80 = 1). So buy 20% less.

8How the examiner asks

Typical style, written by us. Not copied from any paper.

  • "A's salary is 25% more than B's. By what % is B's salary less than A's?"
  • "The price rises by 25%. By what % must consumption (the quantity used) fall so expenditure (money spent) stays the same?"
  • "A number is increased by 20% then decreased by 10%. Find the net change."
  • In data interpretation sets: "what percent is X of Y?" and "% growth from year 1 to year 2". In 2025, DI was reported as the biggest block (8 questions) in memory-based exam analyses, so this skill is used everywhere.

Word clues: "of" means multiply. A percentage point is the plain gap between two percentages: 20% to 25% is +5 percentage points, but also a 25% rise (5 ÷ 20). "than" points to the base. "from … to …" means old to new.

How the wrong options are built. The examiner plants answers from common mistakes: dividing by the new value, adding successive percentages, mixing percentage with percentage points. If your answer matches a "tempting" option too easily, re-check the base.

9Quiz · exam-style marks

Right = +1.25, wrong = −0.3125 (¼ of 1.25; our calculation, some sites say −0.25). Guess only if your chance of being right is above 20%: 0.3125 ÷ (1.25 + 0.3125) = 0.2. After you have crossed out one option, the odds improve.
