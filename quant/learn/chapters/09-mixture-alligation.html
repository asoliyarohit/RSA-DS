Mixture and Alligation

Two lessons: (1) mixing two things and the alligation cross; (2) taking some out and replacing it, again and again. Do one card, then stop if you need to.

Lesson 1: Mix two things, find the ratio (alligation)

In one line: alligation is the cross-over shortcut for mixing. When you mix two things of different strength or price, the result sits between them, and its ratio is the distances on a number line, crossed over.

1Picture this

A kirana (corner grocery) owner in Indore has tea leaves at ₹40 per kg and a costlier lot at ₹60 per kg. She wants a blend that costs her ₹48 per kg. Which lot should she use more of?

40 to 48: gap 848 to 60: gap 12

48 is closer to 40, so the cheaper tea must be the bigger part. The gaps are 8 and 12, and the quantities will be 12 and 8 (crossed): 3 parts cheaper to 2 parts costlier.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

A mixture's price (or strength) is an average (see chapter 06), but a weighted one: the bigger part pulls harder. "Strength" means the % of the key ingredient, like % acid. An alloy is a metal mixture, e.g. silver mixed with copper.
So the mixture value always lies between the two ingredient values. It can never be outside them.
Equal quantities give the plain middle (50 for 40 and 60). Unequal quantities pull it toward the bigger part.
Pure water has 0% acid. Pure ingredient has 100%. These count as ingredients too.
A ratio like 3 : 1 means q1 = 3 parts, q2 = 1 part.
Draw the three numbers: cheaper, mean, dearer. Subtract each from the mean, going diagonally.
The gap on the dearer side tells how much cheaper you need, and the other way round.

3The rules (now that you get it)

Mean = (q1 × v1 + q2 × v2) ÷ (q1 + q2). This is the weighted mean.
Cheaper qty : Dearer qty = (Dearer − Mean) : (Mean − Cheaper)
Check: Mean must lie between the two. If not, you have a slip.

Weighted mean definition: Wikipedia "Weighted arithmetic mean". Alligation (medial = weighted average, alternate = quantities for a target) and the rule Aa + Bb = Cc: Wikipedia "Alligation". Ratio form derived below from the weighted mean and checked by Python.

Why the cross works. Put q1 : q2 as unknowns. Mean × (q1+q2) = q1·v1 + q2·v2. Open the left side: Mean·q1 + Mean·q2 = q1·v1 + q2·v2. Collect q1 terms on the left and q2 terms on the right: Mean·q1 − v1·q1 = v2·q2 − Mean·q2, i.e. q1 (Mean − v1) = q2 (v2 − Mean). So q1 : q2 = (v2 − Mean) : (Mean − v1).

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Tea at ₹40 and ₹60 per kg is mixed to get ₹48 per kg. In what ratio?

Step 1. Cheaper 40, dearer 60, mean 48.
Step 2. Dearer − mean = 60 − 48 = 12. This goes with the cheaper.
Step 3. Mean − cheaper = 48 − 40 = 8. This goes with the dearer.
Step 4. Cheaper : dearer = 12 : 8 = 3 : 2.
Check: (3 × 40 + 2 × 60) ÷ 5 = 240 ÷ 5 = 48. Correct.

How much water must be added to 20 L of a 60% acid solution to make it 40%?

Step 1. Water is an ingredient with 0% acid. Solution 60%, target 40%.
Step 2. Water : solution = (60 − 40) : (40 − 0) = 20 : 40 = 1 : 2.
Step 3. Solution is 20 L = 2 parts, so 1 part = 10 L. Water = 10 L.
Check: acid = 60% of 20 = 12 L. New volume 30 L. 12 ÷ 30 = 40%. Correct.

30 kg of rice at ₹50 per kg is mixed with 20 kg at ₹75 per kg. Price per kg of the mixture?

Step 1. Cost of the first lot = 30 × 50 = ₹1,500. Second lot = 20 × 75 = ₹1,500.
Step 2. Total cost = 1,500 + 1,500 = ₹3,000. Total weight = 30 + 20 = 50 kg.
Step 3. Price per kg = 3,000 ÷ 50 = ₹60. It sits between 50 and 75, nearer 50 because there is more of it.

5The trap

Tea ₹40 and ₹60, target ₹48. A rushed learner writes 8 : 12 = 2 : 3 (gaps not crossed).
Test it: (2 × 40 + 3 × 60) ÷ 5 = 260 ÷ 5 = ₹52. That is not 48, so the ratio is wrong.
Right: cross the gaps. Cheaper gets 12, dearer gets 8, so 3 : 2. A 30-second check is to ask: is the answer's bigger part on the side nearer the mean?

6Mental-math shortcut: the "far gap" rule

Each ingredient gets the far gap (the distance from the mean to the other ingredient). Say "far gap" and the cross is automatic.

Lesson 2: Replacement, again and again

In one line: each time you take out a fraction and replace it with water, the pure part shrinks by the same fraction, so after n times you multiply n times.

1Picture this

A milkman in Jaipur has a 40 L can of pure milk. He pours out 8 L and tops it up with water. Next day he does it again. How much milk is left?

32 L milk8 water

After swap 1: 32 L milk, 8 L water. (One "swap" = pour some out, top up with water.) The second 8 L he takes out is already a mix, so it carries away milk and water.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Total volume V never changes: out x litres, in x litres of water.
Taking out x litres from a well-mixed can removes the fraction x/V of everything in it.
So the pure milk left is the old milk times (1 − x/V). Here 40 × (1 − 8/40) = 40 × 0.8 = 32 L.
Swap again: the fraction taken is still x/V of the new mix. Milk becomes 32 × 0.8 = 25.6 L.
Each swap multiplies the milk by the same factor (1 − x/V). Do it n times: multiply n times.
Water = V − milk. Ratio milk : water comes straight from those two.

3The rules (now that you get it)

Pure left after n swaps = V × (1 − x/V)ⁿ
Fraction of pure left = (1 − x/V)ⁿ. Water = V − pure left.
Adding water without removing: pure stays the same, only the total grows.

The formula is derived in the steps above (multiply by the same factor each time) and checked by Python against step-by-step simulation (derived, Python-checked). Weighted-mean link: Wikipedia "Weighted arithmetic mean".

Link to lesson 1. One swap is itself a mixture: the can (strength 1) and the water (strength 0) in the ratio (V − x) : x, which is the cross method.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

A 40 L can of pure milk: 8 L is drawn out and replaced by water, and this is done twice in all. Milk left? Ratio milk : water?

Step 1. Factor each time = 1 − 8/40 = 1 − 1/5 = 4/5.
Step 2. Milk = 40 × 4/5 × 4/5 = 40 × 16/25 = 25.6 L.
Step 3. Water = 40 − 25.6 = 14.4 L (the total stays 40).
Step 4. Milk : water = 25.6 : 14.4 = 16 : 9 (25.6 ÷ 1.6 = 16 and 14.4 ÷ 1.6 = 9).

A 64 L can of pure milk: 16 L is replaced by water, three times. Milk left?

Step 1. Factor = 1 − 16/64 = 3/4.
Step 2. Milk = 64 × (3/4)³ = 64 × 27/64.
Step 3. The 64 cancels. Milk = 27 L, water = 64 − 27 = 37 L.

A 40 L mixture has milk : water = 3 : 1. 10 L of water is added (nothing removed). New ratio?

Step 1. 3 + 1 = 4 parts = 40 L, so 1 part = 10 L. Milk = 30 L, water = 10 L.
Step 2. Only water is added, so milk stays 30 L. Water = 10 + 10 = 20 L.
Step 3. Milk : water = 30 : 20 = 3 : 2.

5The trap

40 L can, 8 L replaced twice. Wrong move: "8 + 8 = 16 L milk gone, so 24 L left".
Why wrong: the second 8 L contains some water, so less than 8 L of milk leaves. True milk left is 25.6 L, not 24.
Right: multiply by the factor 4/5 twice, 25.6 L. The pure part always falls by less in later swaps.
