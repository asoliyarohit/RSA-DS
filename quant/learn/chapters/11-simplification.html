Simplification and Approximation

Lesson 1: BODMAS, fractions and decimals

In one line: do brackets first, then powers, then ÷ and × left to right, then + and − left to right.

1Picture this

You buy 3 notebooks at ₹20 each and one pen for ₹15, and the shopkeeper says "total = 15 + 3 × 20". You do not add 15 + 3 first and then multiply: you pay for the notebooks (3 × 20 = 60) and then add the pen (15). Total ₹75.

Maths has the same habit: some operations bind tighter than others. Agreeing on the order means everybody gets the same answer.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Work out anything inside brackets first. Nested brackets: inside out.
Then O = "of", as in 3/4 of 240 (it means multiply). Some books read O as "order": powers and roots.
Then division and multiplication. They have equal rank: go left to right.
Last, addition and subtraction. Also equal rank: left to right.
Fractions and decimals are just numbers. Convert them to one form (usually fractions for exact, decimals for quick) before you start.
Fractions with the same bottom number: just add the tops. 3/8 + 5/8 = 8/8, which is 1.
Write one line per step. Do not do two operations in your head at once.

3The rules (now that you get it)

BODMAS = Brackets, Of, Division / Multiplication, Addition / Subtraction
Same rank: work from left to right.
Division and multiplication are NOT "D before M". They are tied. Same for + and −.

Definition of BODMAS (Brackets, Of, Division/Multiplication, Addition/Subtraction) and the left-to-right rule: Wikipedia "Order of operations".

4Worked examples, one tap per step

18 + 6 × (12 − 8) ÷ 4 = ?

Step 1. Bracket first: 12 − 8 = 4. Now: 18 + 6 × 4 ÷ 4.
Step 2. × and ÷ left to right: 6 × 4 = 24, then 24 ÷ 4 = 6.
Step 3. Now 18 + 6 = 24.

0.375 + 5/8 = ?

Step 1. 0.375 has three digits after the point, so it is 375 over 1000. Divide top and bottom by 125 (375 = 3 × 125 and 1000 = 8 × 125): 375/1000 = 3/8.
Step 2. 3/8 + 5/8 = 8/8 = 1.
Check in decimals: 0.375 + 0.625 = 1.000.

5The trap

Question: 8 + 4 × 3.
Wrong move: go left to right for everything: 8 + 4 = 12, 12 × 3 = 36.
Right: multiply first: 4 × 3 = 12, then 8 + 12 = 20.
Second trap: 24 ÷ 4 × 2. Wrong: 4 × 2 = 8 first, 24 ÷ 8 = 3. Right: left to right, 24 ÷ 4 = 6, 6 × 2 = 12.

Lesson 2: Powers, roots and surds

In one line: powers add when you multiply, roots undo powers, and neither splits over addition.

1Picture this

A square tile floor is 12 tiles by 12 tiles. It has 144 tiles. The side (12) is the square root of the area (144). Going from side to area is squaring, and going back is the root.

Powers are repeated multiplication (2 × 2 × 2 = 2³ = 8). Roots undo powers.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

2³ means 2 × 2 × 2 = 8. The small number (the power) says how many times to multiply. The big number being multiplied (the 2) is the base.
Multiplying same-base powers adds the small numbers: 2³ × 2² = 2⁵ (3 + 2 = 5).
Dividing same-base powers subtracts them: 2⁵ ÷ 2³ = 2².
A power of a power multiplies the small numbers: (2³)² = 2⁶.
Anything (not zero) to the power 0 is 1. A negative power is a reciprocal (flip it to 1 over the number): 3⁻² = 1/3² = 1/9.
A square root asks "what number times itself gives this?" The square root of 144 is 12.
A cube root asks "what number times itself three times gives this?" ∛27 = 3, because 3 × 3 × 3 = 27.
A surd is a root that does not come out even, like √2. Keep it as √2 and combine like terms (same root, so just add the front numbers): 5√2 + 3√2 = 8√2. The sign ≈ means "about equal" and ≠ means "not equal".

3The rules (now that you get it)

Here x is the base and m, n are the powers (any numbers).
xᵐ × xⁿ = xᵐ⁺ⁿ  ·  xᵐ ÷ xⁿ = xᵐ⁻ⁿ  ·  (xᵐ)ⁿ = xᵐⁿ
x⁰ = 1  ·  x⁻ⁿ = 1/xⁿ  ·  x^(1/2) = √x
(xy)ⁿ = xⁿyⁿ, so √(ab) = √a × √b. Example: √50 = √25 × √2 = 5√2.

Exponent laws: Math Is Fun "Laws of Exponents". The √(ab) rule follows from (xy)ⁿ with n = 1/2 on that page.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

√50 + √18 = ?

Step 1. Pull out square factors (a square factor is 4, 9, 16, 25 and so on, whose root is whole): 50 = 25 × 2, 18 = 9 × 2.
Step 2. √50 = 5√2 and √18 = 3√2.
Step 3. Add like terms: 5√2 + 3√2 = 8√2. With √2 ≈ 1.414: 8 × 1.414 = 11.312, so about 11.31.

2⁵ × 2³ ÷ 2⁶ = ?

Step 1. Multiply: add the small numbers, 5 + 3 = 8, giving 2⁸.
Step 2. Divide: subtract, 8 − 6 = 2, giving 2².
Step 3. 2² = 4.

5The trap

Wrong move 1: (3 + 4)² = 3² + 4² = 9 + 16 = 25. Right: 7² = 49. Powers do not split over addition.
Wrong move 2: √(9 + 16) = √9 + √16 = 3 + 4 = 7. Right: √25 = 5. Roots do not split over addition either.
Wrong move 3: 2³ × 2² = 4⁵ = 1024. Right: keep the base and add the powers: 2⁵ = 32.
Roots and powers split over multiplication only.

Lesson 3: Approximation: round without losing the option

In one line: round to friendly numbers, solve, and pick the nearest option, but round gently when options are close.

1Picture this

A tea-stall bill: ₹49.80 for tea, ₹20.10 per kg of biscuits for 9.9 kg. You do not need the paise. You think "about ₹50, plus about ₹20 × 10 kg = ₹200". The answer is "close to ₹250" (exact ₹248.79) and you move on.

Approximation questions ask for exactly this: a close answer, fast, picking the nearest option.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Look at the options first. If they are far apart, you can round hard.
Round each number to a friendly nearby number: 49.8 → 50, 20.1 → 20, 9.9 → 10.
Round to nearest ten, whole number or tenth, using the half-up rule: 7.5 goes up to 8, 7.4 goes down to 7.
Then do BODMAS on the friendly numbers.
Pick the option closest to your answer.
If two options are close, round more gently (nearest tenth) and redo.

3The rules (now that you get it)

Round half up: a digit 5 or more rounds up, 4 or less rounds down.
Round each number by a tiny amount, so that the errors stay small.
Nearly 100%, 50%, 25%, 10% are your friends: 24.9% of 801 ≈ 25% of 800 = 200.

Round half up (7.5 rounds to 8; 23.5 to 24): Math Is Fun "Rounding Methods"; Wikipedia "Rounding".

4Worked examples, one tap per step

49.8 × 20.1 ÷ 9.9 + 148 ≈ ?

Step 1. Round: 49.8 → 50, 20.1 → 20, 9.9 → 10, 148 → 150.
Step 2. 50 × 20 ÷ 10 = 100.
Step 3. 100 + 150 = 250. The exact value is 249.11, so an option near 250 is right.

24.9% of 801 ≈ ?

Step 1. 24.9% ≈ 25% = 1/4 and 801 ≈ 800.
Step 2. 800 ÷ 4 = 200. (Exact: 199.449.)

5The trap

Question: 2.6 × 2.6. Wrong move: round both UP to 3 × 3 = 9. The real answer is 6.76, a big miss.
Both numbers rounded up, so the errors stack. Round gently: 2.6 is near 2.5, and 2.5 × 2.5 = 6.25 is far closer than 9. Better still, 26 × 26 = 676, so 2.6 × 2.6 = 6.76.
Also watch: if the options are 199, 200 and 201, rounding to the nearest 10 cannot separate them. Compute more exactly.

7Lab: round and compare

8How the examiner asks

Typical style, written by us. Not copied from any paper.

  • "What approximate value will come in place of the question mark? 398 × 4.02 ÷ 19.9 = ?"
  • "24.9% of 801 + 15.1% of 399 = ?"
  • "√5000 × 0.99 = ?" (approx)

Look for words like "approximately" or "what will come in place of ?". Then you may round; with "exact value" you may not.

How the wrong options are built. They sit around the true value, so if you round too hard you may land between two options. Check the gap between the options before choosing how hard to round.
