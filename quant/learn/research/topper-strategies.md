## 1. Attempt order
- Do quadratic equations, simplification and number series first (7-8 min), then two easier DI sets (10 min), then 4-5 arithmetic word problems (10-12 min), then leftover/difficult DI or arithmetic. [S1] (topper/coaching opinion; single source)
- Take easy and moderate questions first, then difficult ones. [S3, S4, S8] (multiple sources; topper/coaching opinion)
- Tackle Quant and Reasoning last in the whole paper, saving time from easier sections. [S10] (topper/coaching opinion). **Sources differ** with S1 on ordering within Quant and on section order; S10 is about NABARD's sequence of sections.

## 2. Topics to prioritise / leave
- DI (10-15 Qs) plus arithmetic word problems (8-10 Qs) are about 70% of the section; focus there. [S1] Similar emphasis on DI: [S7, S10, S11, S12] (multiple sources)
- Skip lengthy DI sets immediately without attempting. [S1] Complex mensuration and lengthy DI passages: skip initially. [S11] (multiple sources; topper/coaching opinion)
- Prioritise Percentage, Ratio and Proportion, Averages, Mixture and Allegation, Profit and Loss over complex algebra/geometry that rarely appear. [S9] (single source)
- Do not skip arithmetic despite fewer questions per topic; collectively it scores. [S10]. **Sources differ** with S9 on how much to deprioritise geometry/algebra.
- Start with approximation/simplification as the easier topics. [S7] Easy bucket also includes number series, simplification, basic arithmetic. [S11]

## 3. Question selection
- Skip a question that unexpectedly consumes too much time and move on. [S3, S4, S11 ("avoid getting stuck" in S9)] (multiple sources)
- Bucket questions as easy (series, simplification, basic arithmetic), medium (DI sets, profit/loss), difficult (skip initially). [S11]
- In DI, reread questions carefully because unnecessary data is often included; do not assume beyond what is stated. [S3]
- DI: focus on the main figures and trends instead of reading the whole data set. [S4]
- Number series: patterns include primes, squares/cubes, GP, alternating differences or combinations; speed comes from repeated exposure. [S3]
- Avoid guessing because of negative marking. [S10]; negative mark 0.25 per wrong answer noted in [S11]

## 4. Time per question / target attempts
- 1 minute per QA or Reasoning question. [S7, S10] (multiple sources)
- 20 questions in 20 minutes; aim for at least 15 correct (NABARD). [S7]
- RBI Grade B quant of 30 Qs: "18 to 20 accurate attempts" as safe. [S1]. 14-18 good attempts in about 18-22 min. [S11]. **Sources differ** on the target (14-18 vs 18-20) and on the time given.
- Accuracy matters more than raw speed / number attempted. [S1, S11] (multiple sources)
- **Sources differ** on exam pattern: S4 says 60 questions in 45 minutes, S1/S11 describe a 30-question quant section. Check the current notification for your exam.

## 5. Sectional cut-off handling
- Safe approach to clear the sectional cut-off is accurate attempts (18-20 of 30) rather than maximum attempts. [S1] (single source for this framing)
- NABARD enforces sectional cut-offs, so analyse mocks section by section, not just by total score. [S6]
- One page says to score at least 20% in each area to be safe. [S7] (single source, not verified against official notification)

## 6. Speed methods
- Approximation with rounded numbers for percentages, ratios and averages. [S4] (single source in fetched text)
- Memorise percentage-to-fraction conversions. [S7]
- Learn tables, squares, cubes: tables to 30 and squares to 50 [S9]; tables to 20, squares to 40, cubes to 20 [S10]; squares, cubes and percentage-fractions [S1]. **Sources differ** on the exact range; all agree on memorising them (multiple sources).
- Daily mental-calculation practice. [S1, S4, S9] (multiple sources)
- Break multi-step problems into small parts. [S4]
- Keep a formula notebook (speed, work, probability, permutations, CI) and revise daily. [S1]; also revise basic formulas regularly. [S12]
- Practise DI daily or at least on alternate days. [S10]; DI sets with mixed arithmetic logic, 10-15 mixed questions daily. [S12]
- Data sufficiency: test each statement alone, then together; goal is sufficiency, not the answer. [S3]
- Keep conceptual clarity so shortcuts do not become a crutch. [S12]

## 7. Revision plan
- Final 30 days: revise short notes, repeat mocks, practise previous-year papers, prioritise accuracy over new content. [S8] Similar: revise weak topics and old mock questions, 2-3 hours/week, not new content. [S11] (multiple sources)
- Build core concepts first (video/online courses, RS Agarwal), then move to mocks. [S8]. Tanay Gaurav (strong maths background) skipped books and did mostly mocks. [S5]. **Sources differ** on whether books are needed; depends on your base.
- Study quant when mentally sharpest (morning). [S2]
- Previous-year papers to learn patterns. [S4, S7, S8] (multiple sources)

## 8. Mock-test routine
- Full-length mocks in exam-like timed conditions. [S3, S4, S7, S8] (multiple sources)
- Frequency: sectional mock every alternate day in weeks 3-4, full-length daily in the final week [S1]; weekly sectional mocks then full-length from one month out [S11]; full mocks 1-2 per week [S7, S12]. **Sources differ** on frequency.
- Use timed sectional quizzes to build accuracy and pace. [S12]
- Compare the last 5 mocks for accuracy, time distribution and section trends. [S6]

## 9. Error log
- Keep a dedicated error notebook after each mock and review it before the next. [S5 (Tanay Gaurav)], also [S1, S8, S10, S11, S12] (multiple sources)
- Classify mistakes: conceptual gap, calculation mistake, time-management/selection issue [S11]; also calculation errors, misread data [S6].
- Classify each unattempted question: lack of knowledge, time, confidence, or avoidable tough question. [S6]
- Within 24 hours of a test: review 20-30 questions from weakest topics and re-attempt 10-12 wrong ones. [S6] (single source)
- Put new shortcuts/approaches from mock review in a separate notebook. [S1]
- Track time spent per section, per question type and on questions that ended wrong. [S6]
