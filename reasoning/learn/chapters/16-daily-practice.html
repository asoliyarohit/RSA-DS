Daily Practice

In one line: 30 fresh reasoning questions every day, one at a time, and after each answer you see which mistake each wrong option was built from.

Daily Practice

1How to use this (20 to 30 minutes)

  • Same time every day. Do one block of 5, or all six blocks if you have time.
  • Pen and paper ready. Draw the seats, the family tree, the walk. Never hold a puzzle in your head.
  • Skip is allowed. A skip counts 0. Guess only if you can cross out options first (see the 23% rule below).
  • After a wrong answer, read the trap line. It names the mistake behind the option you picked.
  • Next day, tap "Redo my errors" first. Then do the new set.

Same date gives the same 30 questions, so you can come back to them. "Practice again" makes a new set.

2Rules used in every question

  • Seating: everyone sits in one straight row facing north, so their left is your left as you look at the row from behind. Only one seating fits all the clues.
  • Syllogism: take the statements as true. "Some" means at least one, maybe all. Every group named has at least one member. A conclusion follows only if it is true in every diagram that fits the statements.
  • Inequality: a conclusion follows only if it is definitely true. Each question has 4 options (no "either I or II" cases are generated).
  • Coding: the alphabet wraps round, so after Z comes A. "Opposite letter" means A↔Z, B↔Y and so on (27 − position).
  • Blood relations: brother and sister share both parents, and a parent's other child is a brother or sister. A father's wife is the mother, and a mother's husband is the father.
  • Direction: "North-East" means somewhere between north and east, not exactly 45°.

There are no critical-reasoning questions here. A generator cannot promise one defensible answer for them, so that slot is an extra syllogism. Practise critical reasoning in chapter 11.

3Today's set

Order: inequality and syllogism, then coding, blood relations and direction, then input-output and ranking, then series and three seating sets. Blocks of 5 with a short break.

Block done

Stand up, drink a glass of water, then come back.

Break-even about 23%: a wrong answer costs 0.25 and a right one earns 0.8333. A guess pays off only if your chance of being right is above 0.25 ÷ (0.8333 + 0.25), which is about 23%. A blind guess on 4 options is 25%, only just above that. Cross out options first.

4How the score works

Right +0.8333 · Wrong −0.25 · Skip 0

Reasoning has 30 questions for 25 marks, so each right answer is worth 25 ÷ 30 = 0.8333. This guide uses a flat −0.25 per wrong answer, as you asked. The official wording is "1/4th of marks assigned to the question", which would be 0.2083. Your call letter settles it. The 23% rule is safe under both readings.

The topic mix (8 seating, 5 syllogism, 3 inequality, 3 input-output, 3 coding, 2 each of blood relations, direction, ranking and series) is our choice, based on the Priority Map. It is not an official syllabus.

Memorise this
1. Draw first, answer second.
2. Syllogism: it must hold in every diagram.
3. ≥ allows equal, so it never gives a sure >.
4. Total in a row = left rank + right rank − 1.
5. Guess only above about 23% chance.
