Seating Arrangement (linear, circular, two rows)

Lesson 1 · One row (linear)

In one line: a seating puzzle is a row of empty slots; you fill them one clue at a time, and the only tricky word is "left" (it depends on which way the person faces).

1Picture this

Five friends sit in a row on a bench at Lucknow station, all looking at the platform ahead of them. Imagine you stand behind them and look at the backs of their heads. The friend on the far left of your view is also on the left of that friend. Everyone faces the same way you do, so left matches left.

Now picture the same bench with everyone turned to face you. Each person's left is now YOUR right. That one flip causes most seating mistakes.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Draw one box per person, in a line: [1][2][3][4][5]. Number them from the left end of YOUR drawing.
Decide the facing first. In a row, "facing north" means looking up the page. Then their left = the left of your drawing (west) and their right = the right of your drawing (east).
"Immediate left of X" means the box right next to X on X's left. "Between" means strictly inside: the two ends do not count.
"Second from the left end" is box 2. "Second from the right end" in a row of 5 is box 4.
Write the sure clues first (anyone at an end or given a fixed box). Then use "not at" clues to cross out boxes. When one name fits only one box, lock it in.

3The rule (only after the picture)

All face North: their left = West = left of your drawing. All face South: their left = East = RIGHT of your drawing.
Row of n people: k-th from the left = (n + 1 − k)-th from the right.
Strictly between box a and box b (a < b): b − a − 1 people.

Facing north puts east on the right and west on the left: Wikipedia "Cardinal direction". The left/right rule for each heading and the place-from-right formula: derived, Python-checked (coordinates for every heading; all slot counts by enumeration).

4Worked example, one tap per step

Five people A, B, C, D, E sit in a row facing north. C is at the extreme left. E is second from the right end. D is not at the extreme right. A is not at either end. D sits somewhere to the left of A. Find the order, left to right.

Step 1. Draw five boxes: [1][2][3][4][5].
Step 2. C is at the extreme left: C in box 1.
Step 3. E is second from the right end: box 4. E in box 4.
Step 4. Boxes left: 2, 3, 5 for A, B, D. D is not in 5, A is not in 5 (not at either end). So B takes box 5.
Step 5. A and D share boxes 2 and 3. D is to the left of A, so D in 2, A in 3.
Step 6. Answer: C D A E B. Check each clue once. Python brute force over all 120 orders found exactly 1 order that fits.

5The trap

A row faces SOUTH. A person at the extreme east end says "I have a neighbour on my left". Is that possible?
No. Facing south, left = east. At the east end there is nobody further east, so nobody on the left. The neighbour is on his right (west).
Rule: before using "left" or "right", write the compass facing above the row. If the row faces south, flip your drawing: left of the person = right of the drawing.
Python check: with a south-facing row of 4, the east-end seat has left neighbour: none, right neighbour: the next seat to the west.

6Speed method and drill

Four-line method. (1) Boxes. (2) Mark the facing. (3) Fix the sure clues. (4) For every "not" clue, cross boxes out. Skip a question if you have used 90 seconds and nothing is locked (suggested pacing, our judgement).

Elimination shortcut. If an option names a person who is NOT in the box the question asks about, cross it out at once.

Say the answer out loud before you tap Show. Aim: under 5 seconds each.

7Lab: row neighbour finder

8How the examiner asks

Typical style, written by us. Not copied from any paper.

  • "Six persons sit in a row facing north. P is third from the left end and Q is second from the right end. How many persons sit between them?"
  • "Who sits immediately to the left of T?" (read the facing line first).
  • "Which of the following is definitely true?" (an option may be possible but not certain).
How wrong options are built. The examiner plants the neighbour on the wrong side (left and right swapped), the count that includes the two end people, and the option that is only possible, not certain. Use the "definitely" test: true in EVERY valid order.

9Quiz · exam-style marks

Right = +0.8333, wrong = −0.25 (flat, as directed for this guide; the official wording is ¼ of the marks assigned). Guess only if your chance of being right is above about 23%: 0.25 ÷ (0.8333 + 0.25) = 0.231. Crossing out even one option lifts your odds.

10Lock it in

Cover the page, say it out loud: (1) Facing north, which side is left? (2) Facing south? (3) How many people strictly between box 3 and box 7? (4) What do I write before using "left"?
Memorise this
1. Boxes first, facing line second.
2. North: left = west. South: left = east.
3. Between = b − a − 1.
4. k-th from left = (n + 1 − k)-th from right.
5. "Definitely" = true in every valid row.

Lesson 2 · Round table (circular)

In one line: a round table has no start and no end, so fix one person, number the seats clockwise, and remember that facing the centre makes "left" mean clockwise.

1Picture this

Six cousins sit on cushions around a plate of laddoos at a Diwali dinner, everyone facing the plates in the middle. If you walk round the ring the way a clock hand moves, you pass each cousin in turn.

Each cousin has one neighbour on each side and exactly one person straight across the plates (in a ring with an even number of seats).

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Draw a circle with n dots. Number them 1, 2, 3 ... clockwise (the way clock hands move).
Rotating the whole table gives the same seating. So put the first named person in seat 1. This is why a ring has (n − 1)! patterns, not n!.
Facing the centre: stand at the bottom of the circle looking up. Your left hand points west, and walking west from the bottom goes clockwise. So left neighbour = next seat clockwise, right neighbour = next seat anticlockwise.
Facing outside (back to the centre): everything flips. Left neighbour = anticlockwise, right neighbour = clockwise.
Opposite = n ÷ 2 seats away. Only rings with an even number of people have an exact opposite (our stated convention).
Fill sure clues (opposite pairs, fixed gaps), then use "immediate left/right" to add neighbours.

3The rule (only after the picture)

Facing centre: left = clockwise, right = anticlockwise. Facing outside: left = anticlockwise, right = clockwise.
Opposite of seat k in a ring of n (n even) = seat k + n/2 (wrap around).
Rotations count as the same seating, so a ring of n has (n − 1)! patterns.

Clockwise/anticlockwise definition and compass order: Wikipedia "Clockwise", "Cardinal direction". (n − 1)! circular arrangements: Wikipedia "Permutation". Left = clockwise when facing the centre: derived, Python-checked (coordinates, rings of 3 to 12 seats).

4Worked example, one tap per step

Six people A to F sit round a table, all facing the centre. A sits opposite D. B sits immediately to the left of A. F sits immediately to the right of D. C sits immediately to the right of A. Give the order clockwise from A.

Step 1. Draw 6 dots numbered 1 to 6 clockwise. Put A in seat 1 (rotations are equal).
Step 2. Opposite is 3 seats away. D in seat 4.
Step 3. Facing the centre, left = clockwise. B is immediately left of A, so B in seat 2.
Step 4. Right = anticlockwise. F is immediately right of D (seat 4), so go anticlockwise one seat: F in seat 3.
Step 5. C is immediately right of A (seat 1): anticlockwise one seat wraps to seat 6. E takes seat 5.
Step 6. Clockwise from A: A, B, F, D, E, C. Python tried all 720 seatings: 6 fit (the same ring turned 6 ways), so the ring is unique.

5The trap

Six people sit facing the centre. B is immediately left of A. Now everyone turns round to face outside.
Wrong move: "B is still on A's left". The seats did not move, but each person's left and right swapped.
Facing outside, A's left is the anticlockwise neighbour. That is the person who was on A's RIGHT before.
Rule: re-read "facing in" or "facing out" for every clue. Mixed facing (some in, some out) is read person by person: use that person's own facing.

6Speed method and drill

Method. (1) Circle, seats numbered clockwise. (2) Write IN or OUT. (3) Fix the first person, then the opposite person. (4) Add immediate-left/right clues, using the left rule for this facing. (5) Remaining names go in the leftover seats using "not next to" clues.

Elimination shortcut. An option that puts two people in the same seat, or puts the opposite person only 1 or 2 seats away, is wrong at once.

Say the answer out loud before you tap Show.

10Lock it in

Cover the page, say it out loud: (1) Facing the centre, is left clockwise or anticlockwise? (2) Facing outside? (3) How far away is the opposite seat in a ring of 10? (4) Why fix the first person?
Memorise this
1. Number seats clockwise; fix the first person.
2. Facing centre: left = clockwise.
3. Facing outside: left = anticlockwise.
4. Opposite = n/2 seats (even n only).
5. Turn everyone round: left and right swap.

Lesson 3 · Two rows facing each other

In one line: in two parallel rows facing each other, people straight across are "opposite", and the two rows face opposite ways, so "left" points to opposite sides in each row.

1Picture this

Two rows of chairs face each other in a Mumbai office meeting room: the top row looks toward the bottom of your page, the bottom row looks toward the top. A person in the top row and the person straight across from them look into each other's eyes.

When the top-row person lifts her left hand, it points to the same side of the room as the bottom-row person's RIGHT hand.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Draw two lines of boxes, one above the other, lined up. Add compass letters: west on the left of the page, east on the right.
Standard set-up used here: Row 1 (top) faces South. Row 2 (bottom) faces North.
Row 2 faces north: left = west (left of the page). Row 1 faces south: left = east (right of the page).
"Opposite" means the box straight across: same compass position in the other row.
To be safe, give each clue the compass word first, then place the person.

3The rule (only after the picture)

Row facing South: left = East, right = West. Row facing North: left = West, right = East.
Opposite of seat i from the west in one row = seat i from the west in the other row.
Two rows of m people give m opposite pairs.

Compass facing: Wikipedia "Cardinal direction" (facing north: east on the right, west on the left). Row rules and pair count: derived, Python-checked (coordinates; brute force over all seatings).

4Worked example, one tap per step

Rows of three. Row 1 (faces south) holds A, B, C. Row 2 (faces north) holds D, E, F. C sits at the east end of row 1. A sits immediately to the right of C. D sits opposite C. E sits immediately to the left of D. Who sits immediately to the left of A?

Step 1. Draw two rows of three boxes; label west and east.
Step 2. C in the east box of row 1.
Step 3. Row 1 faces south, so right = west. A is immediately right of C: A in the middle, B in the west box.
Step 4. D sits opposite C: D in the east box of row 2.
Step 5. Row 2 faces north, so left = west. E is immediately left of D: E in the middle, F in the west box.
Step 6. Question: left of A. A faces south, so left = east = C. Python brute force over all 720 seatings (names kept in their own rows) found exactly one fit: row 1 B A C, row 2 F E D, west to east.

5The trap

Four people sit in a row facing SOUTH. X sits at the extreme east end. Does X have a neighbour on his left?
Wrong move: "East end is on the right of my drawing, so his neighbour is on his left". The drawing is not his view.
Facing south, his left = east. Nobody is further east. His neighbour is on his right (west).
Same trap across rows: the top row's "immediate left" goes east, the bottom row's goes west. Always translate with compass words.

6Speed method and drill

Method. (1) Two rows of boxes with W and E. (2) Write each row's facing. (3) Place fixed people and opposite pairs first (they fill both rows at once). (4) Translate "left/right" clues with the row's own facing. (5) Count the leftover names.

Elimination shortcut. If an option puts a person in the wrong row, cross it out immediately.

Say the answer out loud before you tap Show.

7Lab: two-row finder

8How the examiner asks

Typical style, written by us. Not copied from any paper.

  • "Eight persons sit in two parallel rows of four. Row 1 faces south, row 2 faces north. ... Who sits opposite C?"
  • "Who sits to the immediate right of the person opposite R?" (two hops: opposite first, then right).
  • "How many persons sit between X and Y?" in a row.
How wrong options are built. The examiner uses the person on the wrong side of the second hop, or the name from the other row. Do the two hops on the drawing, one at a time.

Right = +0.8333, wrong = −0.25 (flat, as directed for this guide; the official wording is ¼ of the marks assigned). Guess only if your chance of being right is above about 23%: 0.25 ÷ (0.8333 + 0.25) = 0.231. Crossing out even one option lifts your odds.

10Lock it in

Cover the page, say it out loud: (1) Which way do row 1 and row 2 face? (2) Left of a south-facing person points where? (3) What is opposite? (4) What do I do before a "left" clue?
Memorise this
1. Row 1 faces South, row 2 faces North.
2. South-facing: left = East. North-facing: left = West.
3. Opposite = same seat number from the west.
4. Two hops: opposite first, then left or right.
5. "Definitely" = true in every valid arrangement.
