Mixed Practice: two 30-question mocks

In one line: the real Reasoning section is 30 mixed questions, so practise exactly that shape, five at a time, and score yourself with the same marks.

Start · How to use the mocks

In one line: one block of 5 is a small win you can finish in about 3 minutes; stack six of them and you have a mock.

1Picture this

In a cricket match nobody bowls 30 balls without a pause: they bowl overs of 6. A mock works the same way. Each block of 5 is one “over”. You finish it, see your score, take a breath, and start the next.

2How to run a mock, one step at a time

Pick one mock (A or B). Do NOT open the chapters while you work. This is a test, not a lesson.
The official paper is 90 questions in one shared 60-minute timer (PFRDA 2026 notification). Our calculation: 60 minutes ÷ 90 questions = 40 seconds per question on average, so 30 reasoning questions is about 20 minutes. That is a suggested pace, not a rule.
Do a block of 5. Tap the answers. Each wrong answer tells you why every wrong option is wrong. Read that before moving on.
After the 6 blocks, add the six block scores and type your total into the marks lab below.
Tomorrow, redo only the questions you got wrong. That second try is where most of the learning happens.

4The rules (marks)

Reasoning: 30 questions, 25 marks, so +0.8333 per right answer (our calculation from the notification table).
Wrong answer: −0.25 (flat, as directed for this guide). Official wording is “1/4th of marks assigned”; the call letter settles it.
Break-even chance of being right = 0.25 ÷ (0.8333 + 0.25) = about 23%.
