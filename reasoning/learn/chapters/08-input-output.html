Input-Output (machine)

In one line: a machine rearranges a line of words and numbers step by step. Learn the move once, simulate it on paper, and every question becomes “write the next line”. Important: every step rule in this chapter is our own typical-style machine, written by us. These are NOT confirmed PFRDA rules; no official PFRDA page gives Input-Output topics or step rules. In the exam, always read the rule off the given steps.

Lesson 1 · One item at a time: sort the line step by step

In one line: a machine fixes ONE item per step. Sort the target order once on paper, and every step is just “bring the next item to its slot”.

1Picture this

A teacher lines up five children by height, one move at a time. First she finds the shortest and pulls that child to the front. The others just shuffle back and keep their order. Next she finds the second shortest and puts that child in place two. That is the whole machine.
Placed (fixed)
Not yet touched

The fixed part grows by one each step.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

An Input-Output question gives an Input line and a rule. The machine changes the line step by step: Step I, Step II, and so on.
A slot means a position in the line: slot 1 is the first place from the left, slot 2 the second, and so on.
Words are put in dictionary order (alphabetical order). Compare first letters; if they match, compare the next letters. A shorter word that is a prefix comes first, so “bat” comes before “bath”.
Numbers are put in size order: ascending means smallest first, descending means largest first.
In our machines, step k takes the k-th item of the sorted target list and PULLS it out of the line. It is put in slot k. The items it jumps over move one place along; all other items keep their order.
Because of that, after step k the line is: the first k sorted items, then everything else in its ORIGINAL order. This is the shortcut that makes options easy to eliminate.
Right-end version: slots are counted from the right. Step 1 puts the item in the far-right slot, step 2 in the slot beside it.

3The rule (now that you get it)

Sort the target list ONCE on rough paper. Step k then only touches slot k.
Shift rule: pull the item out, insert it in slot k, keep the other items in their old order.
Stop count: n items need at most n − 1 steps (the last item settles itself). The machine stops after the last step that changes the line.

Dictionary and shift-versus-swap ideas: selection sort (Wikipedia) describes the same “place the next item in the next slot” loop; its shifting version keeps the other items in order. Dictionary order: Lexicographic order (Wikipedia). The “at most n − 1 steps” and “first k sorted + the rest in original order” facts: derived, Python-checked (all orders of up to 7 items).

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Words: pen map zoo cup bat

Dictionary order: bat cup map pen zoo.
Step I: bat pen map zoo cup (bat goes to slot 1, the rest keep their order).
Step II: bat cup pen map zoo (cup goes to slot 2, the rest keep their order).
Step III: bat cup map pen zoo (map goes to slot 3, the rest keep their order).
No more changes after Step III, so the machine stops. Three steps for five items.

Numbers, smallest first to the left: 42 17 63 8 29

Ascending: 8 17 29 42 63.
Step I: 8 42 17 63 29
Step II: 8 17 42 63 29
Step III: 8 17 29 42 63
Check with the shortcut: after Step II the line is the 2 smallest (8 17) then the rest in original order (42 63 29). It matches.

5The trap

Input: 42 17 63 8 29. Rule: smallest number to slot 1, others keep their order.
Wrong move: SWAP the smallest number (8) with the number in slot 1 (42). That gives 8 17 63 42 29.
Right move: pull 8 out and put it in slot 1; 42, 17, 63 each shift one place right. That gives 8 42 17 63 29.
Test: the rule says the others keep their order. In the swap result, 42 and 63 and 17 are in a different order (17 now before 63 before 42). So swap breaks the rule. Always check Step I against the given line to see which move the machine uses.

6Speed method and elimination

Elimination: for Step k, look only at slots 1 to k. They must equal the first k items of the sorted list. Cross out any option that fails. Then check that the remaining items are in their ORIGINAL relative order.

Routine. (1) Write the sorted list. (2) Cross out options whose first k slots are wrong. (3) Among the rest, find the one whose other items keep input order.

Lesson 2 · Words and numbers together

In one line: two sorts run side by side. Words fill the left end, numbers fill the right end, and the machine alternates between them.

1Picture this

At a railway counter, a clerk keeps tickets by name on the left shelf and cash notes by size on the right shelf. One turn he files the next name on the left; next turn he files the next biggest note on the right. The loose stuff in the middle stays in its old order.
Words
Untouched middle
Numbers

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

A mixed Input has words and numbers jumbled together, for example tea 89 hen 58 elk 55 owl 40.
The rule has two halves: what happens to the words, and what happens to the numbers. It also tells you which goes first and that the two alternate.
Our standard machine: odd steps (I, III, V) bring the next word, in dictionary order, to the next free slot from the LEFT. Even steps (II, IV, VI) bring the next largest number to the next free slot from the RIGHT.
“Next free slot from the right” means: the first number goes to the far right, the second goes BESIDE it, the third beside that.
Everything not yet placed keeps its original order in the middle.
If one kind runs out (say all words are placed), the other kind simply continues on every step.
Shortcut (derived, Python-checked): after each step the line = placed words, then the untouched items in input order, then placed numbers.

3The rule (now that you get it)

Odd step = word to the left. Even step = number to the right (when words go first).
Words: dictionary order. Numbers: largest first, each new one beside the last.
Middle items keep their input order.

Dictionary order: Lexicographic order (Wikipedia). Placing an item and shifting the others while the rest keep order: Insertion sort (Wikipedia). The alternation rule and the prefix + middle + suffix shortcut are our machine, derived, Python-checked (3000 random inputs).

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Input tea 89 hen 58 elk 55 owl 40. Words first.

Words in order: elk hen owl tea. Numbers largest first: 89 58 55 40.
Step I: elk tea 89 hen 58 55 owl 40 (word elk to slot 1).
Step II: elk tea hen 58 55 owl 40 89 (number 89 to the far right).
Step III: elk hen tea 58 55 owl 40 89 (word hen to slot 2).
Step IV: elk hen tea 55 owl 40 58 89 (number 58 beside 89).
Step V: elk hen owl tea 55 40 58 89 (word owl to slot 3).
Step VI: elk hen owl tea 40 55 58 89 (number 55 beside 58).
Step VII would not change anything, so the machine stops after six steps.

5The trap

Input tea 89 hen 58 elk 55 owl 40. Words first. After Step IV the correct line is elk hen tea 55 owl 40 58 89.
Wrong move: send EVERY number to the far-right end, one after another. After Step IV that gives elk hen tea 55 owl 40 89 58.
Spot it: 58 should sit BESIDE 89 (next free slot), not at the very end. 89 is the largest, so it must stay at the far right.
Rule: the first number takes the far right; each later number takes the next free slot to its left.

6Speed method and elimination

Split the Input into two lists first: words and numbers. Sort each. Then write one line per step by only adding one item to the left block (words) or the right block (numbers).

Elimination. After step k, the left block has the first a sorted words and the right block the largest b numbers (a + b = k). Cross out any option where the ends do not match.

Lesson 3 · Find the rule, then predict

In one line: when the rule is hidden, compare Input with Step I and Step II, test each guess by simulating it, and only then predict.

1Picture this

A friend shuffles cards in a way you cannot see. After one shuffle you guess a trick. After two you know. One look can fit two tricks; two looks usually fit one. Input-Output rule-finding works the same way.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

Read Input and Step I. Which item moved? Where did it go: left end, right end, or a slot?
Look at the order of the moved items: ascending or descending, dictionary or length, by size?
Read Step II. Does it repeat the pattern, or switch end, or switch kind (word, then number)?
Guess a rule and SIMULATE it on the Input. If any given line differs, the guess is wrong.
Check shift versus swap: do the jumped-over items keep their order (shift), or does one item just trade places (swap)?
Only after the rule fits every given line, predict the asked step.
Two-ended machine (numbers): odd steps bring the next smallest to the left, even steps bring the next largest to the right.

3The rule (now that you get it)

A rule is right only if it reproduces EVERY given line.
Step I alone can fit shift AND swap. Use Step II.
Word-length order: shortest word first; all lengths must differ for a clear answer.

Sorting orders and stable/shift versus swap: Sorting algorithm, Selection sort (Wikipedia; the shifting version of selection sort keeps other items in order). Two-ended machine and rule-testing routine: derived, Python-checked.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Two-ended machine. Input 82 17 89 36 73 97 78

Smallest first: 17 36 73 78 82 89 97. Largest first: 97 89 82 78 73 36 17.
Step I: 17 82 89 36 73 97 78 (smallest 17 to slot 1).
Step II: 17 82 89 36 73 78 97 (largest 97 to the far right).
Step III: 17 36 82 89 73 78 97 (next smallest 36 to slot 2).
Step IV: 17 36 82 73 78 89 97 (next largest 89 beside 97).
Step V: 17 36 73 82 78 89 97 (next smallest 73 to slot 3).
Step VI: 17 36 73 78 82 89 97 (next largest 82 beside 89).
Step VII leaves the line unchanged: the machine stops after six steps.

Word length. Input plums hen ox bear

Lengths: ox 2, hen 3, bear 4, plums 5. Shortest first.
Step I: ox plums hen bear
Step II: ox hen plums bear
Step III: ox hen bear plums

5The trap

Input 44 11 42 15 46. Step I 11 44 42 15 46.
Guess A: swap the smallest (11) with slot 1. That gives 11 44 42 15 46, the same as Step I.
Guess B: pull it out and shift. That also gives 11 44 42 15 46. Step I cannot tell them apart.
Given Step II 11 15 44 42 46. Guess A would give 11 15 42 44 46. Only guess B fits.
Rule: never fix the rule from one line when two are given.

6Speed method and elimination

Fast test: write each candidate rule as a short line (end, order, move style). Apply each candidate to the Input for ONE step. Cross out candidates that miss Step I. Then apply the survivors to Step II.

Routine. (1) Which item moved, to which end? (2) Order type? (3) Shift or swap? (4) Test on Step II before predicting.
