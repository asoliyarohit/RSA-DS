Start Here: Reasoning game plan

In one line: Reasoning is 30 questions worth 25 marks, a wrong answer costs a quarter mark, and your job is to bank the easy marks first and guess only when the odds are on your side.

Lesson 1 · The numbers and the plan

1Picture this

You are at a mela stall with 30 rings to throw. Each ring that lands scores a little, and each ring that misses costs you a small coin. Smart players throw first at the near pegs, and only gamble on the far pegs when the prize is worth it. The Reasoning section is that stall.

Reasoning is one quarter of Paper 1's 100 marks, packed into 30 questions.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

The Phase I online exam is on 15 October 2026 (Thursday). Paper 1 is for all streams: 90 questions, 100 marks.
Paper 1 has four parts: English 20 Q (25 marks), Quantitative Aptitude 20 Q (25), Test of Reasoning 30 Q (25), General Awareness 20 Q (25).
All four parts share one 60-minute timer (the table shows 60 minutes against Paper 1 as a whole). Average: 60 ÷ 90 = about 40 seconds per question across the paper. That average is our calculation.
Marks per Reasoning question = 25 ÷ 30 = 0.8333. The notification does not print this; it is our calculation from the table.
A wrong answer loses marks. In this guide we use a flat −0.25 per wrong answer (your direction for this build).
Official wording: "1/4th of marks assigned to the question". Taken literally that is 0.8333 ÷ 4 = 0.2083 per wrong answer. The call letter settles which one applies. We use −0.25 everywhere, which is the safer (slightly harsher) reading.
There is a separate cut-off for each paper plus an aggregate cut-off. Phase I marks only shortlist you for Phase II.
Reported in third-party and memory-based pages: through 2025 Paper 1 had 80 questions with 20 in Reasoning. In 2026 Reasoning rose to 30. So it carries more weight than before.

3The rule (now that you get it)

Right = +0.8333. Wrong = −0.25. Skipped = 0.
Break-even chance = 0.25 ÷ (0.8333 + 0.25) = 0.2308, about 23%.
Guess only if your chance of being right is above about 23%. Under the 0.2083 reading the break-even is 20%, so 23% is safe under both.

Exam structure and "1/4th of marks" wording: PFRDA 2026 notification, page 6 (fetched, see Sources). Break-even and marks table: derived, Python-checked. Meaning of probability as "ways it can happen ÷ total outcomes": Math Is Fun "Probability" (fetched).

4Worked examples, one tap per step

You attempt 20 questions: 16 right, 4 wrong. Your Reasoning marks?

Step 1. Right: 16 × 0.8333 = 13.33.
Step 2. Wrong: 4 × 0.25 = 1.00.
Step 3. Marks = 13.33 − 1.00 = 12.33. Skipped 10 questions cost nothing.

You can remove 2 of 4 options. Should you guess between the other 2?

Step 1. Chance of being right = 1 in 2 = 0.5, which is above 23%.
Step 2. Expected marks of one guess (the long-run average) = 0.5 × 0.8333 − 0.5 × 0.25 = 0.4167 − 0.125 = +0.29.
Step 3. Positive, so guess. Compare: with all 4 options open it is 0.25 × 0.8333 − 0.75 × 0.25 = +0.02, barely above zero, and not worth the risk of a bad streak.

5The trap

Thought: "Wrong answers only cost a quarter mark, so I should answer everything."
Check: one wrong answer cancels 0.25 ÷ 0.8333 = 0.3 of a right answer. So 3 wrong answers wipe out about 1 right answer (3 × 0.25 = 0.75, nearly the 0.83 you earned).
Blind guessing on 4 options gets about 1 right in 4. Expected result is about +0.02 per question, so almost zero gain with big swings.
Right move: guess only after you have crossed out at least one option you can prove wrong, and only when your chance is above about 23%.

6Attempt order and how to use this guide

Suggested by us as a strategy, not an official fact.

Pass 1: take the quick, rule-based topics first (inequality, coding-decoding, syllogism, blood relations, direction, order and ranking).
Pass 2: input-output, series and the short logical items.
Pass 3: long puzzles and seating, which eat time. Attempt them last, and only the ones that start cracking in the first minute.
Because the timer is shared across all four parts, decide your Reasoning time budget before the exam. Practise it in the mocks (chapter 15).
How to use this guide: one card at a time. Tap the step button, read one line, stop. One lesson per sitting. Do the quiz, then the cover-and-recall box.
