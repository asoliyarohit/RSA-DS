Order, Ranking, Alphanumeric series

In one line: these questions are all about "who or what stands where", and one tiny habit (count the person once, not twice) wins most of them.

Lesson 1 · Rank in a row

In one line: a person's rank from the left plus their rank from the right counts them twice, so total = left + right − 1.

1Picture this

You queue at a railway ticket window in Lucknow. Someone says "you are 9th from the front". You turn around and count 16 people from the back, counting yourself. How many people in the queue? You are in both counts, so you were counted twice.

8 in frontYou15 behind

8 + 1 + 15 = 24. Left rank 9 and right rank 16 add to 25, one too many.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

A rank (or position) says "which number in the line am I", and you count yourself. First in line is rank 1.
You can count from either end. "9th from the left" and "16th from the right" can describe the same person.
Picture the row with the person in the middle. People in front of them: rank − 1. People behind: the other rank − 1. Add the person: that is the whole row.
So total = left rank + right rank − 1. The −1 removes the double-counted person.
To switch ends for one person: other-end rank = total − rank + 1.
"How many between A and B" means the people strictly in the gap, not A and not B. Put both on the same end, subtract the ranks, then subtract 1.

3The rules (now that you get it)

Total = rank from left + rank from right − 1
Rank from the other end = Total − rank + 1
Between two people = |rank of one − rank of the other| − 1, with both ranks counted from the SAME end
Different people, one rank from the left (a) and one from the right (b): if a + b < Total then between = Total − a − b; if a + b > Total + 1 (the ranks overlap) then between = a + b − Total − 2. If a + b = Total + 1 they are the same person.
Swap: after two people swap, each sits in the other's old place. Translate the new rank into the old place of the other person.

Total, other-end and between formulas: Oliveboard, Testbook, BYJU'S (see Sources). Overlap and non-overlap between rules, same-person case: Testbook, BYJU'S; also derived, Python-checked over every total up to 39 and every pair of ranks.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Ravi is 7th from the left and 12th from the right. How many people?

Step 1. Left rank 7, right rank 12.
Step 2. Add: 7 + 12 = 19. Ravi is counted twice.
Step 3. Subtract 1: 18 people.
Check: 6 in front + Ravi + 11 behind = 18.

Row of 30. A is 8th from the left, B is 12th from the right. How many between?

Step 1. B from the left: 30 − 12 + 1 = 19.
Step 2. Both ranks from the left: 8 and 19.
Step 3. 19 − 8 = 11, then − 1 for B himself: 10 between.
Check with the shortcut: 8 + 12 = 20 is less than 30, so between = 30 − 20 = 10.

5The trap

"9th from the left and 16th from the right. Total?" The tempting answer is 9 + 16 = 25.
That counts the person twice. The right total is 24.
Same trap for "between": 19 − 8 = 11 looks right, but it counts B. Always take 1 more away.
Overlap trap: if the two ranks add to MORE than the total, the people are on the "wrong" sides. Do not use total − a − b (it goes negative). Use a + b − total − 2.

6Speed method and drill

Elimination: the total must be at least as big as each rank. If your answer is smaller than any given rank, it is wrong. Then use total = left + right − 1, which takes about 5 seconds.

Fast order: 1) write the total or the missing rank, 2) put both people on the same end, 3) subtract, 4) take 1 away for "between".

Speed drill

Say the answer before you tap Show. Aim: under 5 seconds each.

8How the examiner asks

Typical style, written by us. Not copied from any paper.

  • "In a row of students, X is 14th from the left and 19th from the right. How many students are in the row?"
  • "In a class of 45, Y ranks 17th from the top. What is the rank from the bottom?"
  • "How many people are between A and B?" with ranks from opposite ends.

Lesson 2 · Alphanumeric series

In one line: a mixed row of digits, letters and symbols is just a numbered row, so number every element and count.

1Picture this

A train coach has seats marked 7, R, #, 3, K and so on. A friend says "go 4 seats to the right of seat number 9". You put a finger on seat 9, then hop 4 times. That is a whole alphanumeric series question.

Start at 9th4 hops → 13th

9 + 4 = 13. The finger spot itself is NOT a hop.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

An alphanumeric series is a row that mixes letters, numbers (digits) and symbols such as # @ $ % & *.
Number every element from the left: 1, 2, 3 ... Write the numbers under the series. This is the single most useful move.
Series of 20? Then the 1st from the right is the 20th from the left. In general, rank from right = total − rank from left + 1, just like Lesson 1.
"4th to the right of the 9th from the left": find the 9th, then go 4 steps right, so you land on 9 + 4 = 13th. "To the left" subtracts instead.
"Immediately followed by" means the very next element. "Between" means strictly inside, not counting the two ends.
Vowels are A, E, I, O, U. Every other letter is a consonant. Digits are 0 to 9. Symbols are everything else.

3The rules (now that you get it)

m-th to the right of the n-th from the left = position n + m from the left
m-th to the left of the n-th from the left = position n − m from the left
n-th from the right = position (total − n + 1) from the left
Elements strictly between positions i and j = j − i − 1

Question types ("n-th from left/right", "m-th to the right of n-th", "immediately preceded/followed", "between"): TutorialsPoint and Unacademy (see Sources). Position arithmetic: derived, Python-checked on the series below.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

Series: 7 R # 3 K @ 9 M $ 2 T 5 & E 8 % L 4 * U (20 elements). Which is the 3rd to the right of the 5th from the left?

Step 1. Number them: 1:7 2:R 3:# 4:3 5:K 6:@ 7:9 8:M 9:$ 10:2 11:T 12:5 13:& 14:E 15:8 16:% 17:L 18:4 19:* 20:U.
Step 2. 5th from the left is K.
Step 3. 3 steps right: @, 9, M. Land on 5 + 3 = 8th: M.

Same series. How many vowels are immediately preceded by a symbol and immediately followed by a digit?

Step 1. List the vowels: E (14th) and U (20th).
Step 2. E: before it is & (symbol), after it is 8 (digit). Both tests pass.
Step 3. U: before it is * (symbol), but U is last so nothing follows. It fails.
Step 4. Answer: 1.

5The trap

"4th to the right of the 9th": the 9th is hop zero. Start your count on the NEXT element.
So hops land on 10, 11, 12, 13. Answer is the 13th, not the 12th.
"To the right" does not flip when the reference is counted from the right. The 6th from the right is still a place in the row. Moving to its left means a smaller position number.
Also check the edge: "followed by" for the last element has no answer, so that element does not count.

6Speed method

1) Write position numbers under the series once. 2) For "how many" questions, circle the target type (for example every letter) first, then check the neighbour rule on each circle. 3) For removed or reversed series, rewrite the series once, then renumber.

Elimination: if the answer must be a symbol, cross out digits and letters in the options. Two options left means a 50% guess, well above the 23% line.

Lesson 3 · Rank by marks, and the alphabet as a row

In one line: class ranks work like a row from the top, and the English alphabet is simply a row of 26 letters.

1Picture this

A school notice board lists class ranks. Anil is 12th from the top in a class of 40. Bhavna is 9 places below him. Top of the list is rank 1, bottom is rank 40. It is the same row as Lesson 1, just standing upright.

Top 11A8B19 below

Anil 12th, Bhavna 12 + 9 = 21st from the top, so 40 − 21 + 1 = 20th from the bottom.

2The idea, one tiny step at a time

"Top" is the left end and "bottom" is the right end. Every rule from Lesson 1 works. Total = rank from top + rank from bottom − 1.
"9 places below" means 9 ranks further down: rank + 9. "9 places above" means rank − 9.
The English alphabet has 26 letters. Treat it as a row: A is 1st from the left, Z is 1st from the right.
Because 26 + 1 = 27, a letter's place from the left plus its place from the right is 27. So the 7th from the right is the 27 − 7 = 20th letter, T.
Letter at the same place from the other end is the "opposite letter": A and Z, B and Y, F and U, K and P.
Handy anchors: E, J, O, T, Y are the 5th, 10th, 15th, 20th, 25th letters. Vowels A E I O U are 1, 5, 9, 15, 21.

3The rules (now that you get it)

Rank from bottom = Total − rank from top + 1
Alphabet: place from left + place from right = 27
Letters between two letters = (bigger place − smaller place) − 1
Two clues, "exactly k between": try both orders (B right of A and B left of A). Each can give a different total.

Top/bottom ranking and minimum-persons idea: Oliveboard, BYJU'S (see Sources). 26 letters and vowels A E I O U: Wikipedia "English alphabet". Sum-27 rule, opposite letters, E-J-O-T-Y and two-case totals: derived, Python-checked.

4Worked examples, one tap per step

A is 8th from the left, B is 12th from the right, exactly 5 between. Possible totals?

Step 1. Case 1: B is right of A. B is 8 + 5 + 1 = 14th from the left. Total = 14 + 12 − 1 = 25.
Step 2. Case 2: B is left of A. B is 8 − 5 − 1 = 2nd from the left. Total = 2 + 12 − 1 = 13.
Step 3. Both are valid rows. If the question says "minimum", answer 13. If "maximum", 25. If it asks "the total" with no more clues, it cannot be fixed.

In the word CHAMPION, how many letter pairs have as many letters between them in the word as in the alphabet?

Step 1. Number the word: C1 H2 A3 M4 P5 I6 O7 N8. Alphabet places: C3 H8 A1 M13 P16 I9 O15 N14.
Step 2. Compare the gaps for every pair. Pairs that match: C and A (1 between both ways), H and N (5 and 5), O and N (0 and 0).
Step 3. Answer: 3 pairs. We tested all 28 pairs by code.

5The trap

"7th from the right end of the alphabet": many people compute 26 − 7 = 19 and land on S.
That gives the number of letters in front of the target, not its place. The place is 27 − 7 = 20, which is T.
The same off-by-one hides in "between": letters between E (5) and R (18) is 18 − 5 − 1 = 12, not 13.
And with two clues, one order is rarely the only order. Check both.

6Speed method and drill

Memorise the anchors E, J, O, T, Y (5, 10, 15, 20, 25). Any letter is within 2 steps of an anchor, so you rarely count from A. For the other end use the sum 27.

Elimination: an answer outside A to Z, or a place number above 26, is wrong immediately. For ranks, an answer larger than the total is wrong.

Speed drill

Say it before you tap Show. Aim: under 5 seconds each.
